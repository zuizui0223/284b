# Frog chorus synchrony cross-dataset synthesis v0.1

## Main result

The rainfall result now has **independent support in two very different acoustic monitoring systems**.

### NAAMP

Standardized 5-minute stop surveys aggregated to route-run level:

- 9,399 runs
- 900 routes
- beta(dryness) = -0.0312
- OR = 0.969 per SD
- 95% OR CI = [0.941, 0.998]
- p = 0.0388

This is a small effect and one complete-route sensitivity narrowly crosses zero.

### FrogID independent validation

Expert-validated short recordings, each already containing at least one calling frog species:

- 40,754 recordings
- 18,174 multi-species recordings
- 1,623 weather cells
- beta(dryness) = -0.1592
- OR = 0.853 per SD
- 95% OR CI = [0.827, 0.879]
- p = 1.13e-24

Both frozen robustness checks pass.

## Current ecological claim

> **Across two independent acoustic monitoring systems, frog species are more likely to overlap in the same short calling window closer to recent rainfall.**

The FrogID result matters mechanistically because its denominator is already an active frog recording. Thus the cross-dataset pattern is not explained solely by rainfall making “some frog” more likely to call.

## What did not happen

NAAMP does **not** support:
- stronger rain synchrony specifically at seasonal shoulders;
- rain-driven densification of pairwise co-calling networks.

So the result is not evidence that rain rewires pairwise species interactions.

A defensible interpretation is **weather-triggered community synchrony**: shared environmental cues transiently compress temporal separation among breeding signals.

## Temperature

Warmer conditions are strongly associated with greater event-level overlap in NAAMP after QC and flexible day-of-year adjustment, but temperature has not received the independent external validation obtained for rainfall.

## Boundaries

No causal rainfall claim. No pairwise facilitation claim. No meta-analytic pooling of effect sizes across NAAMP and FrogID because their sampling units and observation designs differ.
