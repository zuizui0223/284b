# Prior-art novelty check for the 284b target-swap experiment

Status: checked against published literature on **2026-11-15**.
This note is an editorial boundary, not a new empirical endpoint.

## Established before 284b

- **Warren, Glor & Turelli (2008), Evolution 62:2868–2883.**
  DOI https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1558-5646.2008.00482.x .
  Introduced explicit tests of ecological niche *equivalency* versus
  *similarity/conservatism*, with overlap indices including Schoener D and
  statistical comparison against a null distribution. Therefore a comparison
  between different-species niche predictions using D is **not new**.
- **Eckert et al. (2024), Nature Communications 15:7586.**
  DOI https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-51899-1 .
  Compared iNaturalist observations and herbarium specimens in environmental
  niche, diversity and distribution representation. Therefore comparing
  model answers from natural-history and community-science sources is
  **not by itself a novel ecological question**.
- **Wilcox et al. (first online 2025; New Phytologist 2026, 251:721–736).**
  DOI https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.70406 .
  Explicitly compared specimen-based and observation-based biodiversity
  interpretations, including spatial biases and complementarity. The
  existing literature already knows that data sources can change answers.

## What the current experiment actually adds

- A *reproducible operational demonstration* that a source pairing with a
  wrong taxonomic key can fall within a frozen, source-to-source discordance
  envelope, despite independent model fitting and intact marginal outputs.
- A multi-state **authorization contract**, where (i) taxon/key identity,
  (ii) separate answer adequacy, and (iii) numerical cross-source coherence
  are audited *in that order*. A pair with a wrong key is ineligible before
  any D threshold is applied.
- A class-specific missing-key error decomposition and FDF qualification
  distinction from the separate M1 analytic track.

These may be useful methodological **packaging**, engineering and inferential
discipline, but the wrong-key "discovery" is not a new ecological mechanism,
nor evidence that ordinary niche overlap tests lack taxonomic metadata.

## What must not be claimed

- First discovery of environmental niche similarity across different species.
- First comparison of herbarium and public-observation distribution models.
- First demonstration that two niches can look similar.
- Novel statistical test of niche equivalency.
- New ecological-process dependency, necessarily causal relationship,
  or true negative species interaction.
- 280 independent taxa or universal sensitivity from 280 cells.
- That the metadata identity gate's 100% rejection of deliberately mismatched
  names is itself a non-trivial predictive advantage.

## Editorial consequence

The target-swap case fits **Supplementary Material** or a methodological
counterexample, rather than the headline of an Ecology Letters Methods paper.
The main paper requires a demonstrated *incremental benefit* of the full
relation endpoint over existing practice, or a distinctive transfer to
biological relationships whose identity/absence/admissibility is genuinely
difficult to establish. No such prospective independent validation is yet
available. The existing original held-out Level-A outcome remains valuable
as a constrained reproducibility check, but insufficient on its own to
support broad superiority or biological mechanisms.
