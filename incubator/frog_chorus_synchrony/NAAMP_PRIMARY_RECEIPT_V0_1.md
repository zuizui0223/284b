# NAAMP chorus synchrony primary receipt v0.1

The fresh frog programme's first empirical endpoint is now open and frozen.

## Primary result

Across **9,399 standardized NAAMP route runs**, covering **93,383 sampled stops** and **900 routes**, the frozen rain-pulse coefficient was:

- beta = **-0.0312**
- OR per 1 SD increase in `log1p(DaysSinceRain)` = **0.969**
- 95% OR CI = **0.941–0.998**
- cluster-robust p = **0.0388**

The preregistered directional support rule passes.

Because the predictor is days **since** rain, the negative sign means that multi-species chorus synchrony is slightly higher closer to a recent rainfall event.

## Prespecified sensitivities

- runs with >=8 sampled stops: same negative direction, p = **0.0431**, support rule passes;
- complete 10-stop runs: same negative direction, p = **0.0580**, CI narrowly crosses zero.

Therefore the signal is **small and directionally consistent, but not uniformly conventionally significant across all prespecified sensitivities**.

## Current ecological claim

> Recent rainfall is associated with a modest increase in the probability that multiple frog species call during the same standardized listening window.

This is an observational weather association, not evidence that rainfall causally synchronizes frogs.

## What remains prospectively available

The original fresh-programme protocol already declared temperature, seasonal-position dependence, and network reconfiguration as secondary hypotheses. They remain unopened. They can only be opened after their previously specified semantic gates are frozen; they cannot replace the rainfall primary result.
