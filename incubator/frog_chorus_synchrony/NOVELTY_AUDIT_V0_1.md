# Novelty audit v0.1 — frog chorus synchrony

## Core claim

The paper does **not** claim that rainfall makes frogs call, nor that rainfall increases local frog species richness. Both ideas have clear antecedents.

The prospectively defensible claim is:

> Across two independent continental-scale acoustic monitoring systems, more recent rainfall is associated with greater short-window overlap in frog calling across species. In FrogID, the relationship persists conditional on a recording already containing at least one calling species, while a prespecified NAAMP pairwise network test is null.

This combination supports a **shared environmental activation / temporal-niche compression** interpretation rather than a claim of pairwise interspecific facilitation or network rewiring.

## Closest prior art

| Prior work | What it already establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Hsu, Kam & Fellers 2006, *Journal of Zoology*, DOI 10.1111/j.1469-7998.2006.00044.x | In a Taiwanese subtropical forest, the number of calling species and maximum calling intensity covaried with rainfall and air temperature. | Independent continental replication; short-window event-level co-calling; conditioning on an already-active recording. |
| Xie et al. 2017, *Ecological Indicators*, DOI 10.1016/j.ecolind.2017.06.015 | Automated acoustic estimates of community calling activity and species richness were related to rainfall and temperature over one local sensor study. | Cross-system replication and event-level multispecies overlap as the response. |
| Allen-Ankins & Schwarzkopf 2021, *Animal Behaviour*, DOI 10.1016/j.anbehav.2021.07.024 | Calling was aggregated at night/minute scales in a 14-species tropical savannah community; pairwise avoidance appeared at finer call scales. | A replicated weather-dependent community-synchrony effect across continental monitoring systems. |
| Allen-Ankins & Schwarzkopf 2022, *Scientific Reports*, DOI 10.1038/s41598-022-06396-0 | Continental FrogID data support spectral acoustic niche partitioning; temporal call traits were much less partitioned. | Rainfall recency as a predictor of event-level temporal overlap, independently replicated outside FrogID. |
| Greenberg et al. 2023, *Ecology and Evolution* / amphibian community synchrony study | Environmental variation can structure amphibian phenology and within-species synchrony; between-species breeding-migration synchrony was not generally explained by the tested environmental conditions. | Short acoustic-window synchrony under rainfall across independent continental systems. |

## Novel contribution

The new information is the **scale, response definition, conditioning, and replication** together:

1. **Short-window overlap** rather than seasonal richness or first-call phenology.
2. **Community-level co-calling probability** rather than species-specific calling intensity.
3. **Independent designs**:
   - NAAMP: standardized 5-minute route-stop monitoring in North America.
   - FrogID: expert-validated short citizen-science recordings in Australia.
4. **Conditional validation**: FrogID analysis includes only recordings already containing at least one calling frog, so the replicated rain signal is not reducible to rain merely activating any frog call.
5. **Mechanistic falsification boundary**: prespecified NAAMP pairwise network densification is null, arguing against a simple network-rewiring interpretation.
6. **Prospective analysis trail**: rain primary, sensitivities, external validation, and negative secondary endpoints were frozen before their corresponding effects were opened.

## Claims to avoid

Do not write:
- “We show for the first time that rainfall increases frog calling.”
- “Rainfall causes multispecies synchrony.”
- “Rainfall facilitates interspecific interactions.”
- “Rainfall rewires frog acoustic networks.”
- “Frog communities abandon temporal niche partitioning after rain.”
- “The NAAMP and FrogID odds ratios are directly comparable effect-size replicates.”

## Strongest defensible interpretation

> Shared rainfall cues are associated with a transient compression of temporal separation among calling species at the scale of short acoustic observations.

This interpretation is stronger than “rain makes frogs call” because the FrogID validation conditions on acoustic activity already being present, but it remains weaker than a causal claim about interspecific behavioural coordination.
