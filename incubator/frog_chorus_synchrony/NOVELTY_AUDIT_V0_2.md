# Novelty audit v0.2 — frog chorus synchrony

## Revised novelty boundary

This programme does **not** claim that rainfall makes frogs call, that rainfall increases calling-species richness, or that rainfall can synchronize the seasonal onset of frog choruses. Those effects already have clear antecedents.

The strongest defensible claim is narrower:

> **Across two independent continental acoustic monitoring systems, more recent rainfall is associated with greater overlap of calling species within the same short observation window. In FrogID, this relationship persists conditional on a recording already containing at least one calling species.**

The contribution is therefore the combination of **short-window overlap, conditional activation, independent continental replication, and prospective replication rules**.

## Closest prior art

| Prior work | What it already establishes | Remaining distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Hsu, Kam & Fellers 2006, *Journal of Zoology*, DOI 10.1111/j.1469-7998.2006.00044.x | Nightly calling-species richness and maximum calling intensity covary with rainfall and temperature in a Taiwanese forest. | Does not test whether rainfall changes multispecies overlap within the same short acoustic window across independent monitoring systems. |
| Xie et al. 2017, *Ecological Indicators*, DOI 10.1016/j.ecolind.2017.06.015 | Community calling activity and species richness show lagged relationships with rainfall in a local acoustic-sensor study. | Response is activity/richness accumulated over monitoring intervals rather than short-window co-calling conditional on acoustic activity. |
| Allen-Ankins & Schwarzkopf 2021, *Animal Behaviour*, DOI 10.1016/j.anbehav.2021.07.024 | A tropical savanna community can show broad aggregation at night/minute scales while avoiding overlap at finer call scales. | Does not test replicated rainfall recency effects on event-level multispecies calling probability. |
| Allen-Ankins & Schwarzkopf 2022, *Scientific Reports*, DOI 10.1038/s41598-022-06396-0 | FrogID data show stronger spectral than temporal acoustic niche partitioning among co-calling species. | Does not test rainfall recency as a driver of whether a recording contains one versus multiple calling species. |
| Brodie, Allen-Ankins & Schwarzkopf 2025, *Ecosphere* 16:e70153, DOI 10.1002/ecs2.70153 | In three north-Queensland breeding sites over two wet seasons, high rainfall often triggered chorus onset; explosive breeders chorused on the night of or night after the first high-rainfall event, with strong synchrony of initial choruses across sites. | Establishes rainfall-triggered chorusing and onset synchrony, but not replicated short-window co-calling probabilities across independent continental monitoring systems or a validation conditioned on any calling already being present. |

## What is genuinely new

1. **Observation-window scale.** The response is whether multiple species are calling in the same 5-minute NAAMP stop or short FrogID recording, not seasonal richness or chorus onset date.
2. **Conditional validation.** FrogID validation includes only recordings with at least one calling species, separating the replicated signal from the trivial explanation that rainfall only raises the chance of hearing any frog.
3. **Independent monitoring designs.**
   - NAAMP: standardized route-stop surveys in North America.
   - FrogID: expert-validated citizen-science recordings in Australia linked independently to ERA5 rainfall.
4. **Cross-system prospective replication.** The FrogID validation rule and rainfall metric were frozen before validation weather values/effects were opened.
5. **Mechanistic limits are empirical.** Prespecified NAAMP seasonal-shoulder amplification and pairwise network densification were null.
6. **No effect-size pooling.** Different observation designs and rainfall metrics are treated as directional replication, not interchangeable effect sizes.

## Reviewer-safe wording

Prefer:

> Recent rainfall is associated with greater short-window multispecies calling overlap across independent acoustic monitoring systems.

and:

> The replicated association is consistent with shared environmental activation that transiently compresses temporal separation at the observation-window scale.

Avoid:

- “Rainfall synchronizes frog communities for the first time.”
- “Rainfall causes temporal niche collapse.”
- “Rainfall makes species interact.”
- “Rainfall rewires the acoustic network.”
- “This is the first evidence that frog choruses synchronize after rain.”
- “The NAAMP and FrogID odds ratios estimate the same population parameter.”

## Remaining novelty risk

The programme sits close to the literature on weather-driven chorusing and temporal acoustic organization. Its publishable distinction depends on keeping the response explicitly **event-level multispecies overlap**, emphasizing the **conditional FrogID validation**, and treating “temporal-niche compression” as interpretation rather than a directly measured niche-width parameter.
