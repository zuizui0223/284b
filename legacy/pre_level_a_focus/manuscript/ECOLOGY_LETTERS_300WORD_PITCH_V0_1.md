# Ecology Letters Method proposal — 300-word pitch v0.1

Ecologists increasingly combine answers reconstructed from heterogeneous observation sources, model classes, taxa and biological roles, yet two distinct questions are often conflated: whether independent reconstructions are reproducible and whether different biological roles satisfy a dependency that external biology says should hold. We propose a prospective answer-check framework in which the ecological relation, biological event, relation-space adapter, adequacy gates and calibration rule are frozen before focal outcomes are opened. The framework allows role-specific estimators and accessible areas; comparability is created only after answers are projected onto a common biological event space.

We demonstrate the controlled case with independently reconstructed ecological answers from preserved-specimen and human-observation records. A successor panel prospectively froze 24 procedure-by-accessible-area reference cells before a 12-taxon held-out endpoint was opened. Of 288 held-out cells, 283 passed both source-specific adequacy gates and all 283 remained within their frozen source-discordance ceilings; five remained unresolved rather than being recoded as agreement or disagreement.

We then prospectively stress-test escalation to hard cross-role dependency. Finite candidate screens and source-architecture gates identify two fresh systems with independently separated relation, dependent-event and required-function evidence streams. Both stop before focal biological opening because their observation processes do not independently identify functional absence, and existing supplementary/raw sources do not reconstruct that missing calibration.

This yields a general negative-state identifiability principle: a hard ecological dependency can be falsified by a positive dependent event and a negative required function only when the negative functional state is itself identifiable under a prospectively calibrated observation process. Missingness, sensor failure and unvalidated non-detection are therefore not biological absence. The framework separates cross-source reproducibility from cross-role dependency and provides transferable rules for interaction ecology, host dependence, life-stage coupling, sensor ecology and distribution-based process inference.
