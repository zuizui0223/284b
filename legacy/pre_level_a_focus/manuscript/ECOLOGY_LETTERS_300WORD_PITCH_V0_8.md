# Ecology Letters Method proposal — 300-word pitch v0.8

Detection-aware occupancy models already separate ecological state from imperfect observation, and the LIES framework systematizes observation-process problems of Latency, Identifiability, Effort and Scale. We address a different inferential object: when independently generated ecological answers are combined, what biological relation are they entitled to test, and when may that endpoint be opened? We introduce an executable relation-endpoint contract that freezes the relation, biological key space, role-specific adapters, answer-adequacy gates and opening rule before focal outcomes are viewed. The complete contract, including its calibration/protocol reference, is canonically serialized and SHA-256 fingerprinted before focal opening.

Classical coupling bounds show why this layer is not reducible to better marginal models: even with exact `p_E=p_F=0.5`, the hard-violation probability `P(E=1,F=0)` can range from 0 to 0.5 under different joint couplings.

As a controlled empirical anchor, 12 fresh held-out taxa generated 288 prespecified same-target procedure-by-area diagnostics from independent specimen and human-observation reconstructions. Of these, 283 were evaluable and all remained within prospectively frozen empirical source-discordance envelopes; five stayed unresolved. The cell count is diagnostic rather than independent replication.

For hard dependency `E(k) -> F(k)`, the negative function state must itself be identifiable. An exact ablation shows that coercing invalid observations into biological negatives inflates false violations by `1-a1` and apparent violation sensitivity by `1-a0`, where validity may differ by latent function state. Unqualified negatives remain unresolved.

Two prospective cross-role systems with separated relation, event and function streams independently reached the same measurement boundary because functional absence lacked calibration. The method therefore turns relation choice, answer adequacy and negative-state identifiability into an auditable stopping rule for when new measurement, rather than stronger interpretation of the same data, is required. The lead author works across empirical pollination ecology, species-distribution modelling and reproducible computational inference.
