# Ecology Letters Method compliance checklist v0.2

Checked against current Ecology Letters author guidance and the Method editorial on 2026-09-11.

## Proposal stage

- Article type: **Method**.
- Full Method manuscripts are considered only after direct invitation or approval of an unsolicited proposal.
- Unsolicited proposal maximum: **300 words**.
- Proposal should describe nature, novelty, disciplinary contribution and author qualifications.
- Editorial guidance explicitly notes that quantitative information and/or a conceptual figure can strengthen a proposal.
- Proposal should be sent to both `ecolets@cefe.cnrs.fr` and `ecolets2@cefe.cnrs.fr`.
- Canonical pitch: `ECOLOGY_LETTERS_300WORD_PITCH_V0_5.md`.
- Canonical package: `ECOLOGY_LETTERS_METHOD_PROPOSAL_V0_5.md`.
- Canonical attachment: `figures/ecology_letters_method_proposal_figure_v0_2.svg`.
- Email wrapper: `ECOLOGY_LETTERS_PROPOSAL_EMAIL_V0_1.md` (content synchronized to v0.5; final author details still required).

## Full manuscript constraints if invited

- Method main text maximum: **5,000 words**.
- Maximum display items: **6** total figures/tables/text boxes.
- Method abstract maximum: **150 words**.
- Method papers should introduce a specific generalizable technique transferable across systems and research questions.
- Quantitative methods require high-quality code.
- The method should be the focus and should be demonstrated in a case study.
- Review is based on novelty and importance of the method.
- Title page requires article type, author information, running title <45 characters, up to 10 keywords, abstract/main-text word counts, reference count and display-item counts.
- Data/code accessibility statement and final repository DOI(s) are required for the publication package.
- A graphical abstract is required at revision stage under current guidance.

  4. answer-adequacy gates;
  5. calibration/opening rule.

## Prior-work boundary now required

The proposal/manuscript must explicitly acknowledge rather than appropriate the following antecedents:

- imperfect-detection and occupancy/co-occurrence modelling;
- Chadwick et al. (2024) **LIES** framework (Latency, Identifiability, Effort and Scale);
- ecological model adequacy/data determinacy (Getz et al. 2018);
- independent external validation;
- preregistration and Registered Reports;
- the generic principle that non-detection is not biological absence.

The retained novelty claim is **relation-endpoint authorization across independently generated ecological answers**.

## Internal go/no-go checks before sending proposal

1. <=300-word pitch passes automatically.
2. Author-qualification sentence(s) must match the final frozen author list.
3. Figure v0.2 must be exported to a broadly viewable attachment format if the editorial office does not accept SVG.
4. Keep the Level-A claim conditional on answer adequacy: 283/283 opened cells within frozen ceilings; 5 inadequate cells unresolved.
5. Do not open focal Level-C values to strengthen the proposal.
6. Do not describe Level-C calibration unavailability as a biological negative.
7. Keep the exact benchmark framed as endpoint-decision performance, not as a replacement for occupancy/LIES.
8. Keep calibration distinct from false-discovery control.
9. Make any final author-list or affiliation changes without changing the frozen scientific thresholds or endpoint semantics.
10. Before full manuscript submission, create an immutable archive/release with hashes and DOI(s).

## Editorial-risk diagnosis after LIES audit

**Highest remaining risk:** editors may still regard relation-endpoint authorization as a framework rather than a sufficiently specific Method. The defense is now concrete: a five-part contract, executable opening gate, exact `1-a` performance identity, fresh held-out case study and real-system stopping test.

**Second risk:** the exact algebra may be judged simple. The paper therefore should not sell `1-a` alone as the invention. Its role is a transparent performance property of the endpoint authorization rule.

**Third risk:** Level C may look unfinished. The manuscript must continue to say that Level C is a completed *pre-field identifiability stress test*, not a completed biological dependency test. The fact that the method refuses to manufacture a result is part of the validation.

**Fourth risk:** case-study breadth. Counter with the 12-taxon Level-A held-out experiment, two biologically distinct Level-C architectures and explicit transfer examples across pollination, host dependence, trophic interactions, stage coupling and sensor ecology.
