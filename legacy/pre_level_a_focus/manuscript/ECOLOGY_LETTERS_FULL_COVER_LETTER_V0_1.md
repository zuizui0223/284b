Dear Editors,

Please consider our Method manuscript, **“Prospective validation of ecological answers: from cross-source reproducibility to identifiable biological absence,”** for publication in *Ecology Letters*.

Ecologists increasingly combine answers generated from different observation sources, taxa and biological roles, but the relation that those answers are entitled to test is often left implicit. We introduce an executable **relation-endpoint contract** that freezes five objects before focal comparison: the biological relation, the biological key/event space, role-specific adapters, answer-adequacy gates and the endpoint-opening rule. The method sits above any particular ecological estimator. Occupancy models, species-distribution models, phenology models, sensor classifiers or direct measurements may supply role-specific answers; the contract determines what joint biological statement those answers may support and which observations must remain unresolved.

The manuscript contributes three complementary forms of evidence. First, a fresh controlled Level-A experiment uses 12 independently held-out taxa to test same-target reconstruction from preserved-specimen and human-observation records. The 288 prespecified procedure-by-area cells are explicitly treated as repeated diagnostics rather than independent replication; 283 were evaluable, five remained unresolved at adequacy and no held-out taxon contained an empirical-envelope exceedance among its evaluable cells. Second, for hard directional dependency `E(k) -> F(k)`, we derive exact class-conditional operating characteristics for an ablation that coerces invalid observations into biological negatives. With `a1=P(valid|F=true)` and `a0=P(valid|F=false)`, false-violation inflation is exactly `1-a1` and apparent true-violation sensitivity gain is exactly `1-a0`; the familiar `1-a` identity is only the equal-validity special case. Third, two biologically distinct prospective systems—pollination function in *Cremastra* and live-oak resource dependence in *Belonocnema*—independently reach the same endpoint-openability boundary: relation, event and function streams are available, but the negative functional state is not candidate-specifically calibrated. No focal Level-C biological outcome is opened in this paper.

The closest antecedents are explicitly treated as such rather than recast as new. Occupancy and interaction models already address imperfect detection; Chadwick et al. (2024) systematize observation-process problems through the LIES framework; Getz et al. (2018) formalize ecological model adequacy and data determinacy; independent external validation and preregistration are established practices. Our contribution is narrower: **relation choice and endpoint authorization across independently generated ecological answers**, implemented as a tested state machine with exact performance decomposition and auditable stopping rules.

We believe the paper fits the Method format because it provides a specific transferable technique, open tested code, quantitative performance characterization, a fresh empirical case study and real-system stress tests. The framework applies beyond the motivating systems to host dependence, trophic interactions, life-stage coupling, sensor-based interaction studies and distribution-based process attribution.

All focal Level-C biological values remain prospectively sealed. The completed Level-A endpoint is the sole empirical-ledger increment in the pre-field paper. Synthetic and design audits do not count as new biological conclusions. The manuscript, figures, code and frozen receipts are version-controlled, and an immutable archival release/DOI will be supplied before final submission.
