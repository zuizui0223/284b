Dear Editors,

Please consider our Method manuscript, **“Relation endpoints for ecological inference: when independent answers can support joint biological claims,”** for publication in *Ecology Letters*.

Ecologists increasingly combine ecological answers generated by different observation processes and biological roles. Existing occupancy, data-fusion, joint-distribution, observation-process and preregistration frameworks address important upstream problems, but a downstream question remains: **which biological relation are independently defensible answers licensed to test together, and what evidence is sufficient to open or contradict that relation?** We introduce an executable relation-endpoint contract that prospectively freezes the biological relation, common event/key space, role-specific adapters, answer-adequacy gates and endpoint-opening rule. The complete contract includes the referenced calibration or negative-state protocol and can be canonically serialized and SHA-256 fingerprinted before focal opening.

The need for this layer is not a claim of new probability theory. Classical Fréchet–Hoeffding bounds show that even perfect role-specific marginals need not identify a directional dependency: at `P(E=1)=P(F=1)=0.5`, the same marginal answers permit hard-violation probability `P(E=1,F=0)` from 0 to 0.5. Joint models can estimate statistical coupling; the relation-endpoint problem is the separate biological authorization of what that coupling is entitled to represent and what observation state can contradict it.

We support the method with one fresh empirical anchor, one exact operating-characteristic result and two real-system stopping-rule demonstrations. Twelve independent held-out taxa were evaluated under a prespecified same-target cross-source relation. Their 288 repeated diagnostics yielded 283 evaluable cells and five unresolved cells, and **0 of 12 taxa contained an exceedance of the prospectively frozen empirical source-discordance envelopes**. The cell diagnostics are not treated as independent replication. For hard endpoints, preserving an explicit unresolved state has an exact class-specific consequence: coercing invalid observations into biological negatives increases false-violation probability by `1-a1` and apparent true-violation sensitivity by `1-a0`, where observation validity may differ between compatible and true-violation states.

Finally, two biologically distinct prospective systems—pollination function in *Cremastra* and live-oak resource dependence in *Belonocnema*—independently reach the same endpoint-openability boundary. Both satisfy relation/event/function architecture requirements, but the negative functional state is not candidate-specifically calibrated. The framework therefore refuses the hard biological inference and identifies the next admissible information as new calibration or direct functional measurement. **No focal Level-C biological dependency outcome is opened in this paper.**

We position the contribution narrowly. Occupancy and detection modelling, joint species distribution modelling, ecological data fusion, co-occurrence/interaction inference, the LIES observation-process framework, model adequacy, external validation and ecological preregistration are treated explicitly as antecedents rather than inventions of this paper. Our contribution is the executable **relation-authorization layer** linking independently generated ecological answers to a prospectively declared joint biological claim, together with auditable stopping rules for when that claim may not yet be made.

We believe the manuscript fits the Method format because it provides a transferable technique, open tested code, deterministic endpoint specifications, exact performance characterization, a fresh held-out empirical demonstration and biologically different real-system stress tests. The main manuscript is deliberately concise; audit/provenance detail, the full synthetic grid, calibration-feasibility calculations and candidate/source qualification are retained in an auditable Supplement rather than used to inflate the main empirical claim.
