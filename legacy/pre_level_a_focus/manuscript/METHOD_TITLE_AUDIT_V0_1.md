# Ecology Letters Method title audit v0.1

## Problem

The current working title is:

**Prospective validation of ecological answers: from cross-source reproducibility to identifiable biological absence**

It accurately describes the paper's arc, but after the relation-layer separation audit it has one editorial weakness: the title does not name the paper's distinctive methodological object, **relation endpoints**. The phrase "identifiable biological absence" can also make the paper sound more like a detection/non-detection method than it is.

## Candidate titles

### A — current

**Prospective validation of ecological answers: from cross-source reproducibility to identifiable biological absence**

Pros: readable narrative; covers Level A to hard-negative identifiability.  
Risk: novelty object is hidden; may invite the wrong comparison class (occupancy/detection methods).

### B — method-object first

**Relation endpoints for ecological inference: when independent answers can support joint biological claims**

Pros: names the new methodological object immediately; states the inferential problem; does not imply a completed Level-C biological result; distinguishes the paper from data fusion and detection modelling.  
Risk: "relation endpoint" is a new term and must be defined immediately in the abstract.

### C — architecture first

**Relation-endpoint authorization across independent ecological answers**

Pros: precise and compact.  
Risk: sounds abstract/computational and less recognizably ecological; weaker narrative hook.

### D — prospective method first

**A prospective relation-endpoint method for ecological inference across independent answers**

Pros: makes prospectivity and method explicit.  
Risk: generic and less memorable than B.

## Recommendation

**B is the strongest desk-review title after the separation argument.**

It exposes exactly what is new without claiming that occupancy, detection, JSDMs, data fusion, coupling theory or non-detection logic are new. It also fits the paper's executable output: a contract that determines when independent answers can support a declared joint biological claim.

Recommended title:

> **Relation endpoints for ecological inference: when independent answers can support joint biological claims**

## Promotion rule

Do not silently replace the canonical title in already frozen submission files. Promote the new title only together across the pitch, proposal email, title page and invited manuscript, with CI checking exact-title synchronization.

Changing the title does not change any scientific threshold, endpoint, empirical result or ledger state.
