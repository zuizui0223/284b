## Discussion

### Relation authorization is a distinct layer of ecological inference

The main contribution of the relation-endpoint framework is not a new estimator. It is a rule for deciding what joint biological claim independently generated ecological answers are authorized to support. Occupancy and detection models can improve the identifiability of latent ecological states; data-fusion methods can combine heterogeneous sources; JSDMs can estimate joint statistical structure; LIES clarifies observation-process limitations; model-adequacy frameworks evaluate whether data support an intended model; and preregistration can protect analysis choices from outcome-dependent revision. The relation endpoint sits downstream of these tools. It specifies the biological relation, the common opportunity at which that relation applies and the observation state sufficient to open or contradict it.

This distinction matters because answer quality and relation identification are not the same problem. The classical coupling bounds used here make that separation explicit: even exact role-specific marginals can support radically different values of a hard-violation probability. The bounds themselves are established mathematics and are not a contribution of this paper. Their role is diagnostic. They show that improving upstream marginal answers cannot, by itself, determine whether the biological relation of interest is satisfied. Joint statistical modelling may narrow the coupling uncertainty, but a biological endpoint still has to declare what type of joint structure counts as support or contradiction.

The practical consequence is that standardization should occur at the level of the **relation**, not necessarily at the level of the upstream models. Different biological roles may require different estimators, predictors, accessible areas or sampling designs. Forcing them onto identical modelling machinery can produce a cleaner computational comparison while weakening the biology. The relation-endpoint contract instead allows role-specific answers to remain defensible on their own terms and requires explicit adaptation only at the event where biology makes a prediction.

### Level A provides an empirical anchor rather than a universal reproducibility theorem

The same-target experiment shows that a prospectively specified relation can close empirically on fresh data without outcome-dependent rescue. Across 12 independent held-out taxa, no taxon contained an exceedance of the frozen source-discordance envelope among its evaluable cells. The 283 evaluable cells are repeated diagnostics generated by a prespecified procedure-by-area matrix, not 283 independent biological replicates. Five cells remained unresolved because answer adequacy failed, and the framework preserves them as unresolved rather than converting them into evidence against the relation.

This result should be read narrowly and usefully. It does not show that specimen and human-observation reconstructions are universally source invariant, nor that the nearest-rank q95 envelope is a nominal 95% prediction interval. It shows that, for the declared same-target estimand and frozen analysis matrix, a relation defined before opening can survive a fresh held-out test across 12 taxa. That controlled closure is important because it demonstrates that relation-endpoint authorization is not merely a language for refusing inference: when the required states are identified and the calibration is defined, an endpoint can close.

The result also clarifies why the same Level-A envelope should not be exported to cross-role problems. Source discordance for two reconstructions of the same target is a different object from biological discordance between roles that may legitimately occupy different spaces or times. Cross-role endpoints therefore require relation-specific calibration rather than a universal similarity threshold.

### Unresolved states prevent a specific inferential failure

Hard dependency makes the status of observational zeros central. A contradiction to `E(k) -> F(k)` requires evidence for `E(k)=true` and evidence for `F(k)=false` at the same biological opportunity. If the observation process cannot distinguish functional absence from non-detection, missingness or failure, then the negative premise is not identified. The appropriate state is unresolved.

The generalized ablation quantifies the cost of violating this rule. When observation validity can depend on latent function state, coercing invalid observations into biological negatives increases false violations by exactly `1-a1` and apparent sensitivity to true violations by exactly `1-a0`. Equal inflation is therefore not universal; it occurs only in the equal-validity special case `a1=a0`. The importance of the result is not mathematical complexity. It isolates the decision consequence of deleting the unresolved state.

This framing also avoids a misleading comparison with modern detection-aware models. Zero collapsing is not proposed as a realistic competitor. It is a controlled removal of one guardrail. Occupancy, interaction or sensor models can satisfy the negative-state gate when their sampling design identifies the relevant state. The relation endpoint then consumes that qualified answer. When the observation process does not identify the state, however, a more elaborate fit to the same information cannot automatically authorize a hard negative.

Process qualification is also not equivalent to final evidentiary reliability. Sensitivity and specificity determine whether a negative state is eligible to enter a hard endpoint, but the false-discovery fraction among called violations also depends on the prevalence of true violations and on how evidence is aggregated across keys. A later confirmatory Level-C analysis therefore needs separately frozen replication and aggregation rules even after the observation process passes calibration.

### Refusal is a scientific result when the stopping rule is prospective

The two Level-C systems are deliberately not presented as incomplete dependency tests. They are real-system stress tests of endpoint openability. Both retained systems passed biological-relation definition, common-key alignment, direct event/function estimands and evidence separation. Both then stopped at the same point: functional absence was not independently calibrated.

That stop is informative because it was reached prospectively. The endpoint was not weakened after seeing a difficult observation process, candidate systems were not replaced after failure, and non-detections were not reclassified as biological absences. The framework therefore identifies a concrete missing measurement rather than manufacturing a conclusion from an ambiguous zero. In these systems, the next admissible information is candidate-specific calibration or direct measurement of the aggregate function.

This is the strongest role for Level C in the present paper. No focal Level-C dependency is confirmed or falsified. Instead, the examples demonstrate that the same machinery that authorizes a Level-A closure can refuse a harder biological claim for an explicit, measurable reason. A method for ecological synthesis should be judged partly by whether it can prevent overinterpretation when the required state is not identified.

### Scope, limitations and generality

The relation-endpoint contract cannot guarantee that a chosen biological relation is correct. External biological knowledge must justify the relation before it is frozen. Nor does the contract repair misspecified upstream models: answer adequacy remains an upstream responsibility. Calibration thresholds are design choices rather than universal constants, and the present reference implementation directly covers calibrated soft relations and directional hard implication; mutual dependencies and life-stage transitions require relation-specific compositions rather than a generic one-size-fits-all evaluator.

The framework is nevertheless portable because its abstraction is biological rather than taxonomic. Host-dependent development requires a compatible resource during a developmental opportunity, not generic host occurrence. Pollination dependence concerns effective pollination during a reproductive opportunity, not the presence of one named visitor species. Trophic dependence concerns accessible prey at a relevant foraging opportunity, not equality of predator and prey distributions. Life-stage coupling concerns a transition-compatible state at a transition event. Sensor-based interaction studies require observation processes calibrated strongly enough that non-observation can support the negative state demanded by the endpoint.

Across these cases, the workflow is the same: construct role-appropriate answers, map them to the biological relation where a prediction applies, preserve unresolved states, and verify that the state needed to contradict the relation is identifiable before allowing hard inference. The broader methodological claim is therefore modest but consequential: **independently defensible ecological answers support a joint biological claim only after the relation connecting them is prospectively specified and the observation state required to open or contradict that relation is itself identifiable.**
