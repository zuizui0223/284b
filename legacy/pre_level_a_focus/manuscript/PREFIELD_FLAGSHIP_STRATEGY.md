# Pre-field flagship strategy

Status frozen from `bdc81d0` (2026-09-11). This manuscript is prepared before any Level-C v8 field-calibration data entry.

## Scientific object

The paper is not a report of a completed Level-C biological dependency test. It is a general ecology paper about when independently constructed ecological answers can be combined into a defensible cross-check, what can be learned from a fresh held-out same-target control, and why hard cross-role dependency inference fails unless the negative functional state is itself identifiable.

The empirical anchor is the completed Level-A endpoint `same_target_cross_source_reproducibility_heldout12_core19_v0_4`: 283/283 opened held-out cells remained within prospectively frozen successor-derived source-discordance ceilings; 5 cells stayed closed at the predeclared answer-adequacy gate.

The second contribution is a prospectively documented escalation from same-target cross-validity to cross-role dependency. Level-C v2-v8 shows that repairing the biological relation from named-provider occurrence to event-to-function dependency is not sufficient by itself. Two fresh systems reached separated relation/event/function evidence architectures, but hard inference remained closed because a non-detection could not be prospectively identified as `F(k)=false`. Existing supplementary/raw sources did not repair that calibration gap, fixing the current boundary at new candidate-specific measurement rather than candidate hunting or reinterpretation.

## Frozen one-sentence claim

**Independent ecological answers can support prospectively calibrated cross-validity when target and estimand are controlled, but a hard cross-role dependency cannot be opened merely from independent positive streams: the required negative functional state must itself be prospectively identifiable under a calibrated observation process.**

## Claims explicitly forbidden in the pre-field paper

- No claim that Level C has produced a biological positive or negative endpoint.
- No claim that Cremastra or Belonocnema violates or confirms the hard dependency.
- No recoding of unavailable, missing, retrieval failure, device failure, observer failure, occlusion, or unresolved adjudication as biological absence.
- No process-causal or necessity claim; process knockout remains closed.
- No use of the Level-A q95 ceiling as a cross-role tolerance.
- No claim of universal source invariance or equality of raw suitability values.
- No post hoc candidate replacement, threshold relaxation, or rescue of screened systems.

## Journal ladder

### Stretch target: Ecology Letters — Method

Why it is the highest defensible first shot:
- The paper proposes a conceptual/methodological framework for ecological inference, not merely a taxon-specific workflow.
- The fresh Level-A held-out result provides an empirical demonstration rather than a purely programmatic essay.
- The Level-C sequence exposes a general inferential boundary with broad relevance to interaction ecology, distribution modelling, multi-source ecology, and process attribution.
- The manuscript can be kept concise and hypothesis-driven.

Submission route: unsolicited Method proposal first, then full manuscript only if invited. Do not pitch it as a completed plant-pollinator result. Pitch the general problem: independent ecological answers are often combined without first specifying the relation space or identifying the negative state required for falsification.

### Primary fallback: Methods in Ecology and Evolution — Research Article

Strong scope fit because the method is conceptual and intended for broad uptake. Risk: MEE explicitly deprioritizes workflows that simply link existing methods and generally expects simulation or benchmark testing for computational methods. Therefore the manuscript must establish that the novelty is the prospective relation/identifiability architecture itself, with synthetic qualification tests and the Level-A benchmark, not a wrapper around SDMs.

### Conceptual fallback: The American Naturalist — Major Article

Strong fit if the manuscript is written around a general conceptual issue: ecological dependencies are only falsifiable when the required negative state is observationally identifiable. The method must be shown to advance a broad conceptual problem, with Level A as the empirical control and Level C as the boundary demonstration.

### Lower-priority route: Global Ecology and Biogeography — Method

Viable only if the manuscript is reframed strongly around macroecological/biogeographic inference from distributional data. Current 284b framing is broader than biogeography, so this is not the preferred first route.

## Paper architecture

### 1. Problem
Ecologists increasingly combine independently reconstructed answers from heterogeneous sources and roles. Agreement is often interpreted without first asking what relation should hold, what is allowed to differ, and whether the states needed to falsify the relation are observable.

### 2. Prospective relation ladder
Define same-target cross-source replication, soft cross-role concordance, directional dependency, mutual dependency, and life-stage coupling. State that cross-role comparability is created only after role-specific answer construction through a frozen relation-space adapter.

### 3. Controlled empirical test: Level A
Use preserved-specimen versus human-observation records for the same targets while holding target, estimand, estimator/procedure, M semantics, comparison rows, predictor universe, adequacy gates, and reference rule fixed. Report the 24/24 calibrated reference cells and 283/283 held-out cross-valid cells; keep five inadequate cells unresolved.

### 4. Why same-target success cannot be exported upward
Show formally/conceptually why a source-disagreement envelope is not a biological tolerance for different roles or estimands. Introduce event-key dependency rather than raw-surface containment.

### 5. Level-C stress test of identifiability
Summarize the prospectively frozen finite screens and the transition from named-provider occurrence to `E(k) -> F(k)`. Report design/qualification states, not focal biological outcomes. Two systems ultimately reach separated relation/event/function channels but stop at inability to identify hard functional absence.

### 6. The negative-state identifiability principle
State the general result: hard dependency testing requires both an adequately positive dependent event and an adequately negative required-function state under a prevalidated observation rule. Missingness or non-detection without calibrated detection adequacy is not a falsifying observation.

### 7. Consequences
Explain implications for species interactions, host dependence, pollination, stage coupling, occupancy/distribution modelling, sensor ecology, and any workflow that interprets absence of evidence as evidence of functional absence.

## What would materially strengthen the pre-field manuscript without opening Level C

- A compact synthetic benchmark showing false conclusions induced by treating non-detection as functional absence under varying detection sensitivity/missingness.
- A negative-control demonstration that the Level-A q95 cannot be safely exported to cross-role comparisons.
- A worked toy example showing two valid role-specific estimators mapped into the same relation space.
- A machine-readable claim/evidence ledger linking every manuscript statement to a frozen artifact.
