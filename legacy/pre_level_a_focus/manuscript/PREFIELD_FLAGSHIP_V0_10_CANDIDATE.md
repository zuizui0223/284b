# Relation endpoints for ecological inference: when independent answers can support joint biological claims

**Target:** Ecology Letters — Method  
**Status:** noncanonical editorial candidate v0.10 — main-text compressed; frozen v0.7 and Stage-1 package remain authoritative until separately promoted.  
**Frozen empirical boundary:** Level A closed; Level B unopened; Level C focal biological outcomes sealed.  
**Empirical ledger:** 1.

## Abstract

Ecological synthesis increasingly combines answers generated by different observation processes and biological roles, but accurate answers do not by themselves specify the biological relation they may jointly test. We introduce an executable relation-endpoint contract that prospectively freezes the relation, biological key space, role-specific adapters, adequacy gates and opening rule. Twelve fresh held-out taxa provided a controlled empirical anchor: 283 of 288 prespecified same-target diagnostics were evaluable, five remained unresolved, and no taxon contained an exceedance of its frozen empirical source-discordance envelopes. Classical coupling bounds show that even exact role-specific marginals need not identify a directional dependency. For hard endpoints, coercing invalid observations into biological negatives inflates false violations by `1-a1` and apparent violation sensitivity by `1-a0`. Two independent cross-role systems reached the same stopping point because functional absence was not calibrated. Relation endpoints separate answer construction from biological authorization and identify when new measurement is required.

## 1. Introduction

Ecological inference increasingly depends on combining answers generated by different observation processes. Species distributions may be reconstructed from preserved specimens, opportunistic observations, structured surveys and sensors; interaction partners may be represented by separate streams; and different life stages or functional roles are often estimated at different spatial and temporal grains. Independent sources can strengthen evaluation, but they also create a downstream question that is easy to hide inside modelling choices: once two ecological answers are individually defensible, **what biological relation are those answers entitled to test together?**

Several mature frameworks address important parts of this problem. Occupancy and detection models separate latent ecological state from observation (MacKenzie et al. 2004; Guillera-Arroita 2017). Multispecies and joint species distribution models can represent joint occurrence and distinguish marginal from joint prediction (Rota et al. 2016; Wilkinson et al. 2021). Data-fusion methods integrate heterogeneous sources into common latent inference (Pacifici et al. 2017). Interaction studies distinguish missed interactions from non-occurrence, model-adequacy frameworks ask whether available data support an intended model, and the LIES framework makes Latency, Identifiability, Effort and Scale explicit properties of ecological observation processes (Weinstein & Graham 2017; Getz et al. 2018; Chadwick et al. 2024). External validation and ecology-specific adaptive preregistration likewise provide established tools for testing predictions and protecting analyses from outcome-dependent redesign (Gould et al. 2026). We do not claim novelty for any of these ingredients.

The missing object is a **relation endpoint**: the prospectively declared biological relation that independently generated ecological answers are jointly authorized to evaluate. This object is distinct from answer construction. Two reconstructions of the same target can often be compared directly because target, estimand and comparison frame can be held fixed. Different biological roles usually cannot. A perennial plant may persist after local pollinator loss; an adult insect can occur outside larval resource patches; predator and prey distributions need not be equal even when prey availability constrains foraging. Forcing role-specific models onto the same raw scale, predictor set or accessible area can therefore make the comparison less biological rather than more standardized. The relevant comparison must instead be defined at the event where biology predicts a relation.

Accurate upstream answers do not eliminate this relation layer. For a directional dependency `E -> F`, let `p_E=P(E=1)` and `p_F=P(F=1)` be perfectly known role-specific marginals and let `v=P(E=1,F=0)` be the probability of a hard violation. Classical Frechet-Hoeffding coupling bounds imply

`max(0,p_E-p_F) <= v <= min(p_E,1-p_F)`.

Thus exact `p_E=p_F=0.5` is compatible with both `v=0`, where the implication is never violated, and `v=0.5`, where every positive `E` key violates it (Nelsen 2006). This is established probability theory used here only as a separation argument: improving marginal answer accuracy alone cannot identify the hard relation. A suitable JSDM can estimate joint statistical structure (Wilkinson et al. 2021), but co-occurrence or residual association is not by itself evidence of a biological interaction (Blanchet et al. 2020). Conversely, co-occurrence can contain information about interaction-network structure under explicit assumptions (Galiana et al. 2024). The remaining question is therefore not whether joint statistical information can be estimated, but **which biological relation that information is licensed to represent, at what event key, and what state counts as support or contradiction**.

Hard dependency also exposes a second boundary. If event `E(k)` requires function `F(k)` at biological opportunity `k`, then observing `E(k)=true` with `F(k)=false` can contradict the dependency. But an observational zero is not automatically `F(k)=false`. It can arise from incomplete coverage, imperfect detection, missingness or observation failure. Detection-aware models can identify latent state when sampling contains the information required to do so; when it does not, a hard contradiction cannot obtain its negative premise by relabelling non-detection as absence. The endpoint therefore needs an explicit unresolved state.

We introduce an executable, estimator-agnostic **relation-endpoint contract** that freezes five objects before focal comparison values are opened: (1) the biological relation, (2) the common biological event or key space, (3) role-specific adapters into that space, (4) answer-adequacy gates, and (5) the calibration or opening rule that determines which endpoint states are admissible. The complete contract, including the frozen artifact or protocol that instantiates the opening rule, can be canonically serialized and fingerprinted before focal opening. The fingerprint is an audit mechanism rather than proof of outcome blindness; its purpose is to make endpoint drift inspectable.

We evaluate the framework in four complementary ways. First, a controlled same-target experiment provides a fresh empirical anchor: 12 prospectively held-out taxa were evaluated across a prespecified analysis matrix, with repeated cells treated as diagnostics rather than independent replication. Second, the coupling argument formalizes why individually accurate answers do not determine the downstream biological relation. Third, an exact state-dependent ablation quantifies the consequence of coercing invalid observations into biological negatives. Finally, two independent cross-role ecological systems are carried prospectively through the same authorization logic and stop at the same measurement boundary because functional absence is not independently calibrated. Together these analyses ask not only whether ecological answers can be produced, but when they are scientifically licensed to compose into a joint biological claim.

## 2. Methods

### Relation-endpoint contract

We treat the relation joining independently generated ecological answers as an explicit inferential object rather than an implicit consequence of upstream model choice. Before focal comparison values are viewed, a relation-endpoint contract freezes five scientific components: (1) the biological relation, (2) the event or key space at which it applies, (3) role-specific adapters mapping each answer into that space, (4) adequacy gates determining whether each answer exists for a key, and (5) an opening rule defining admissible endpoint states. The implementation also stores an `opening_rule_reference` identifying the frozen empirical envelope, calibration artifact or negative-state qualification protocol that instantiates that rule.

The reference engine (`scripts/relation_endpoint_contract.py`) validates the contract, serializes it to deterministic canonical JSON and returns a SHA-256 fingerprint. Missing or unknown fields are rejected. Fingerprinting makes endpoint drift inspectable; it is an audit device, not proof of outcome blindness. Calibrated soft keys return `consistent`, `attention_required` or `unresolved`. Directional hard keys return `hard_violation_authorized`, `no_observed_violation`, `noninformative_for_implication` or `unresolved`. Implementation and round-trip fingerprint audits are in Supplement S2.

### Why answer accuracy does not identify the relation

For directional dependency `E -> F`, let `p_E=P(E=1)` and `p_F=P(F=1)` be exact role-specific marginals and `v=P(E=1,F=0)` the hard-violation probability. Classical Fréchet–Hoeffding coupling bounds imply

`max(0,p_E-p_F) <= v <= min(p_E,1-p_F)`.

At `p_E=p_F=0.5`, the same exact marginals admit both `v=0` and `v=0.5`. We use this established result only as a separation argument: marginal accuracy does not determine the downstream hard relation. Joint statistical models may estimate coupling; the biological relation, common key and contradiction rule must still be declared separately.

### Controlled same-target empirical anchor

We tested whether a fully prespecified soft relation could close on fresh held-out taxa using independent preserved-specimen and human-observation reconstructions. Within each procedure-by-accessible-area cell, target identity, estimand, predictor universe, estimator/procedure identity, accessible-area semantics, comparison frame, adequacy logic and the successor-derived reference rule were held fixed before held-out opening.

The independent biological units were 12 held-out taxa. Each taxon contributed a prespecified 3-area × 8-procedure matrix, yielding 288 repeated diagnostics. A diagnostic was evaluable only when both source-specific answers passed predeclared adequacy. Discordance was `1 - Schoener D` and was compared with the corresponding frozen nearest-rank q95 **empirical source-discordance envelope**. The 24-cell calibration, successor counts, comparison-frame construction and cell-level diagnostic receipts are reported in Supplement S1. q95 is not interpreted as nominal 95% predictive coverage or a hypothesis-test threshold.

### Directional dependency and negative-state identifiability

For a hard relation we write `E(k) -> F(k)`, where `E(k)` is the dependent event and `F(k)` the required biological function at opportunity key `k`. A hard contradiction requires an adequately positive event and an adequately negative function at the same key. When several providers can satisfy the requirement, `F` is defined at the aggregate function level rather than as occurrence of one named provider.

Detected function, calibrated functional absence, unresolved non-detection and observation failure/missingness are distinct states. Only calibrated functional absence can supply `F(k)=false`. The frozen Level-C process-qualification floor requires key-level sensitivity `>=0.80`, hard false-negative rate `<=0.20` and specificity `>=0.95`, with explicit quality-control and missingness rules. These thresholds authorize whether a negative state may enter the endpoint; they are not universal final standards for a biological conclusion. The prospective confidence-bound repair and calibration-feasibility details are in Supplements S4–S5.

### Exact unresolved-state ablation

To isolate the consequence of deleting `unresolved`, we compared the identifiability-gated hard rule with a zero-collapsing ablation that deliberately treats invalid observation or valid non-detection as `F=false` when `E=true`. This is an ablation of one guardrail, not a competitor to occupancy or other detection-aware models.

Let `q` be key-level sensitivity on valid compatible keys, `sp` specificity on valid true-violation keys, `a1=P(valid key | F=true)` and `a0=P(valid key | F=false)`. Then

`FPR_zero - FPR_gated = 1-a1`

and

`TPR_zero - TPR_gated = 1-a0`.

The common `1-a` identity is the equal-validity special case `a1=a0=a`. Full derivations, the 8,748-scenario stress grid and the base-rate/false-discovery analysis are in Supplement S3.

### Prospective cross-role openability audit

We tested whether real cross-role systems could satisfy the information requirements of a directional endpoint without opening focal biological outcomes. Each candidate required prospectively separated relation evidence (`R`), dependent-event data (`X`) and required-function/resource data (`Y`). Candidate discovery was finite and response-blind; gates required a common biological key, direct role estimands, provenance separation and no post hoc redefinition. Unavailable sources, failed downloads, missing records and uncalibrated zeros were never allowed to count as biological absence.

Two systems survived the architecture gates: `CREMV3-007` (*Cremastra appendiculata* var. *variabilis*) and `BELV3-012` (*Belonocnema treatae* / live-oak budbreak). Paper 1 uses them only to test endpoint openability. Their focal hard invariant, soft cross-check and process-knockout outcomes remain sealed. The full candidate screen, source qualification and raw/supplementary-source audits are in Supplement S6.

## 3. Results

### A prospectively frozen same-target relation closed on 12 fresh taxa

Across the 12 prospectively held-out taxa, **0 of 12 contained an empirical-envelope exceedance among evaluable cells**. Their prespecified analysis matrices produced 288 repeated diagnostics; both independently reconstructed answers passed adequacy in 283, while five remained unresolved. Each taxon contributed 22–24 evaluable diagnostics.

The 283/283 count is a repeated diagnostic summary, not an independent sample size. The result supports same-target cross-source coherence conditional on answer adequacy and the prospectively frozen empirical source-discordance envelopes; it does not imply exact source invariance, nominal 95% predictive coverage or a general tolerance for cross-role relations. Cell-level margins are reported in Supplement S1.

### Accurate marginals did not determine the hard relation

For `E -> F`, the same exact marginals `p_E=p_F=0.5` permit both `v=P(E=1,F=0)=0` and `v=0.5` under the classical coupling bounds. Thus perfect role-specific marginal answers can support opposite hard-relation status. This nonempirical separation result motivates the downstream relation object; it is not claimed as new probability theory.

### Deleting `unresolved` created exact class-specific error increments

With state-dependent observation validity, zero collapsing increased false violations by exactly `1-a1` and apparent sensitivity to true violations by exactly `1-a0`. For illustration, at `a1=0.70`, `a0=0.90`, `q=0.95` and `sp=0.99`, false-violation probability rises from 0.035 under the gated rule to 0.335 under zero collapsing, while apparent true-violation sensitivity rises from 0.891 to 0.991. Equal increments occur only under the equal-validity special case `a1=a0`.

When process calibration failed, the gated rule emitted no hard-negative calls. Observation-process qualification nevertheless did not guarantee that a called violation was biologically true; endpoint-level reliability additionally depends on the prevalence of true violations and on separately frozen replication/aggregation rules. Full grid results and base-rate calculations are in Supplement S3.

### Two real systems stopped at the same negative-state measurement boundary

Finite response-blind screening retained two biologically different systems with separated relation, event and function/resource evidence. `CREMV3-007` combined external breeding-system evidence, same-inflorescence fruit outcome and pollinia-carrying camera observations for *Cremastra appendiculata* var. *variabilis*. `BELV3-012` combined independent host-specificity evidence, natural adult emergence and live-oak budbreak observations for *Belonocnema treatae*.

Both passed source materialization, common-key alignment, direct estimands, provenance separation and no-post-hoc-redefinition gates. Both then stopped before focal endpoint opening because the negative functional state was not independently calibrated. Camera non-detection was not validated as absence of effective pollination, and an unrecorded usable-resource state could not be distinguished from incomplete observation. Existing identified supplementary/raw sources did not reconstruct the missing candidate-specific calibration.

These are **measurement-boundary results, not biological negatives**. No focal Level-C dependency is confirmed or falsified in Paper 1. For both systems, the next admissible information is new candidate-specific calibration or direct measurement of the required aggregate function. Full qualification and source-reconstruction audits are in Supplement S6.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Relation authorization is a distinct layer of ecological inference

The main contribution of the relation-endpoint framework is not a new estimator. It is a rule for deciding what joint biological claim independently generated ecological answers are authorized to support. Occupancy and detection models can improve the identifiability of latent ecological states; data-fusion methods can combine heterogeneous sources; JSDMs can estimate joint statistical structure; LIES clarifies observation-process limitations; model-adequacy frameworks evaluate whether data support an intended model; and preregistration can protect analysis choices from outcome-dependent revision. The relation endpoint sits downstream of these tools. It specifies the biological relation, the common opportunity at which that relation applies and the observation state sufficient to open or contradict it.

This distinction matters because answer quality and relation identification are not the same problem. The classical coupling bounds used here make that separation explicit: even exact role-specific marginals can support radically different values of a hard-violation probability. The bounds themselves are established mathematics and are not a contribution of this paper. Their role is diagnostic. They show that improving upstream marginal answers cannot, by itself, determine whether the biological relation of interest is satisfied. Joint statistical modelling may narrow the coupling uncertainty, but a biological endpoint still has to declare what type of joint structure counts as support or contradiction.

The practical consequence is that standardization should occur at the level of the **relation**, not necessarily at the level of the upstream models. Different biological roles may require different estimators, predictors, accessible areas or sampling designs. Forcing them onto identical modelling machinery can produce a cleaner computational comparison while weakening the biology. The relation-endpoint contract instead allows role-specific answers to remain defensible on their own terms and requires explicit adaptation only at the event where biology makes a prediction.

### 4.2 Level A provides an empirical anchor rather than a universal reproducibility theorem

The same-target experiment shows that a prospectively specified relation can close empirically on fresh data without outcome-dependent rescue. Across 12 independent held-out taxa, no taxon contained an exceedance of the frozen source-discordance envelope among its evaluable cells. The 283 evaluable cells are repeated diagnostics generated by a prespecified procedure-by-area matrix, not 283 independent biological replicates. Five cells remained unresolved because answer adequacy failed, and the framework preserves them as unresolved rather than converting them into evidence against the relation.

This result should be read narrowly and usefully. It does not show that specimen and human-observation reconstructions are universally source invariant, nor that the nearest-rank q95 envelope is a nominal 95% prediction interval. It shows that, for the declared same-target estimand and frozen analysis matrix, a relation defined before opening can survive a fresh held-out test across 12 taxa. That controlled closure is important because it demonstrates that relation-endpoint authorization is not merely a language for refusing inference: when the required states are identified and the calibration is defined, an endpoint can close.

The result also clarifies why the same Level-A envelope should not be exported to cross-role problems. Source discordance for two reconstructions of the same target is a different object from biological discordance between roles that may legitimately occupy different spaces or times. Cross-role endpoints therefore require relation-specific calibration rather than a universal similarity threshold.

### 4.3 Unresolved states prevent a specific inferential failure

Hard dependency makes the status of observational zeros central. A contradiction to `E(k) -> F(k)` requires evidence for `E(k)=true` and evidence for `F(k)=false` at the same biological opportunity. If the observation process cannot distinguish functional absence from non-detection, missingness or failure, then the negative premise is not identified. The appropriate state is unresolved.

The generalized ablation quantifies the cost of violating this rule. When observation validity can depend on latent function state, coercing invalid observations into biological negatives increases false violations by exactly `1-a1` and apparent sensitivity to true violations by exactly `1-a0`. Equal inflation is therefore not universal; it occurs only in the equal-validity special case `a1=a0`. The importance of the result is not mathematical complexity. It isolates the decision consequence of deleting the unresolved state.

This framing also avoids a misleading comparison with modern detection-aware models. Zero collapsing is not proposed as a realistic competitor. It is a controlled removal of one guardrail. Occupancy, interaction or sensor models can satisfy the negative-state gate when their sampling design identifies the relevant state. The relation endpoint then consumes that qualified answer. When the observation process does not identify the state, however, a more elaborate fit to the same information cannot automatically authorize a hard negative.

Process qualification is also not equivalent to final evidentiary reliability. Sensitivity and specificity determine whether a negative state is eligible to enter a hard endpoint, but the false-discovery fraction among called violations also depends on the prevalence of true violations and on how evidence is aggregated across keys. A later confirmatory Level-C analysis therefore needs separately frozen replication and aggregation rules even after the observation process passes calibration.

### 4.4 Refusal is a scientific result when the stopping rule is prospective

The two Level-C systems are deliberately not presented as incomplete dependency tests. They are real-system stress tests of endpoint openability. Both retained systems passed biological-relation definition, common-key alignment, direct event/function estimands and evidence separation. Both then stopped at the same point: functional absence was not independently calibrated.

That stop is informative because it was reached prospectively. The endpoint was not weakened after seeing a difficult observation process, candidate systems were not replaced after failure, and non-detections were not reclassified as biological absences. The framework therefore identifies a concrete missing measurement rather than manufacturing a conclusion from an ambiguous zero. In these systems, the next admissible information is candidate-specific calibration or direct measurement of the aggregate function.

This is the strongest role for Level C in the present paper. No focal Level-C dependency is confirmed or falsified. Instead, the examples demonstrate that the same machinery that authorizes a Level-A closure can refuse a harder biological claim for an explicit, measurable reason. A method for ecological synthesis should be judged partly by whether it can prevent overinterpretation when the required state is not identified.

### 4.5 Scope and generality

The relation-endpoint contract cannot guarantee that a chosen biological relation is correct. External biological knowledge must justify the relation before it is frozen. Nor does the contract repair misspecified upstream models: answer adequacy remains an upstream responsibility. Calibration thresholds are design choices rather than universal constants, and the present reference implementation directly covers calibrated soft relations and directional hard implication; mutual dependencies and life-stage transitions require relation-specific compositions rather than a generic one-size-fits-all evaluator.

The framework is nevertheless portable because its abstraction is biological rather than taxonomic. Host-dependent development requires a compatible resource during a developmental opportunity, not generic host occurrence. Pollination dependence concerns effective pollination during a reproductive opportunity, not the presence of one named visitor species. Trophic dependence concerns accessible prey at a relevant foraging opportunity, not equality of predator and prey distributions. Life-stage coupling concerns a transition-compatible state at a transition event. Sensor-based interaction studies require observation processes calibrated strongly enough that non-observation can support the negative state demanded by the endpoint.

Across these cases, the workflow is the same: construct role-appropriate answers, map them to the biological relation where a prediction applies, preserve unresolved states, and verify that the state needed to contradict the relation is identifiable before allowing hard inference. The broader methodological claim is therefore modest but consequential: **independently defensible ecological answers support a joint biological claim only after the relation connecting them is prospectively specified and the observation state required to open or contradict that relation is itself identifiable.**
