# Prospective validation of ecological answers: from cross-source reproducibility to identifiable biological absence

**Target:** Ecology Letters — Method  
**Stage:** pre-field manuscript v0.4  
**Frozen empirical boundary:** Level A closed; Level B unopened; Level C focal biological endpoint unopened  
**Pre-data implementation repair:** Level-C v8.1 exact-confidence evaluator  
**Synthetic benchmark:** `pre_field_identifiability_benchmark_v0_1`  
**Main-text target:** <= 5,000 words  
**Display-item target:** <= 6

## Abstract

Ecologists increasingly combine answers reconstructed from different observation sources and biological roles. Existing occupancy and interaction models show why imperfect detection must be separated from ecological state, model-adequacy frameworks ask whether data support intended inference, and independent datasets are recognized as stronger tests of predictive models. A different problem arises when multiple ecological answers are combined to support a biological relation: what relation are those answers entitled to test, and when is the state required to contradict it identifiable? We develop a prospective relation-endpoint framework in which the relation, biological event, relation-space adapter, answer-adequacy gates and calibration are frozen before focal outcomes are opened. In a controlled same-target test, 283/283 adequately observed held-out cells reconstructed independently from preserved-specimen and human-observation records remained within prospectively calibrated source-discordance ceilings; five inadequate cells remained unresolved. For hard cross-role dependency `E(k) -> F(k)`, we require the negative function state `F(k)=false` itself to be identifiable. An exact 8,748-scenario benchmark shows that, for a qualified observation process with valid-key fraction `a`, collapsing incomplete, missing or failed keys into zero increases both apparent violation sensitivity and false-violation probability by exactly `1-a`. A second analytic result shows that calibration is an endpoint-opening condition, not false-discovery control. Prospective cross-role screens identified two fresh systems with independent relation, event and function streams, but both remained sealed because functional absence was not independently calibrated. The framework separates reproducibility from biological dependency and provides a stopping rule for when additional modelling should give way to new measurement.

## 1. Introduction

Ecological synthesis increasingly combines answers that were not generated by the same observation process. Species distributions are reconstructed from museum specimens, opportunistic observations, structured surveys and sensors; interacting taxa are represented by different data streams; and host, pollinator, consumer and life-stage responses are estimated at distinct spatial and temporal grains. Independent data are widely regarded as a stronger basis for external evaluation than internal reuse of model-fitting data, and community-science observations have been used as independent evaluation sources for distribution models (Matutini et al. 2021; Gaier & Resasco 2023). At the same time, specimen and observation records are known to differ in sampling and collection processes (Erickson & Smith 2021). Multiple independent sources therefore create both an opportunity for stronger validation and a problem of deciding what kind of agreement is scientifically meaningful.

Imperfect detection is not a new problem. Occupancy models separate latent ecological state from observation and show that non-detection can create false absences (MacKenzie et al. 2004; Guillera-Arroita 2017). Multispecies extensions model co-occurrence while accounting for imperfect detection (Rota et al. 2016), and interaction-network work has asked whether unobserved interactions were truly absent or merely missed (Weinstein & Graham 2017). Likewise, model adequacy is an established concern: Getz et al. (2018) argued that ecological models should be evaluated with explicit attention to data determinacy, sensitivity and validity. Preregistration and Registered Reports have independently established the value of freezing hypotheses and analyses before outcomes are known. We therefore do not claim novelty for imperfect detection, external validation, model adequacy or prospective workflow freezing themselves.

Our target is different. We ask how independently constructed ecological answers can be promoted from model outputs to a **prospectively testable relation**. Two reconstructions of the same biological target can be compared as a controlled cross-source problem because target and estimand can be held fixed. Two different biological roles generally cannot be required to have identical fitted values, suitability surfaces or accessible areas. A perennial plant can persist after local pollinator loss, an adult stage can disperse beyond larval habitat, and a dependent consumer can be observed outside the interval in which its resource was measured. Cross-role inference therefore requires a relation defined at the biological event where the dependency operates rather than a generic comparison of raw surfaces.

Hard dependency adds a further requirement. Suppose a dependent event `E(k)` requires a biological function `F(k)` at opportunity `k`. Observing `E(k)` together with `F(k)=false` can contradict the proposed dependency. Yet a recorded zero is not automatically `F(k)=false`; it can arise from incomplete temporal coverage, imperfect detection, missing records, sensor failure or observer failure. Detection-aware models can estimate latent state when the sampling design identifies the observation process. But when candidate data do not provide that information, a hard biological contradiction cannot acquire its negative premise merely by fitting a more elaborate model.

We develop a prospective validation architecture with three separable tasks. First, independent answers must exist and pass their own adequacy criteria. Second, they must be projected onto a relation space in which external biology makes a specific prediction. Third, when the prediction is a hard dependency, the negative state needed for contradiction must itself be identifiable under a calibrated observation process. We demonstrate the controlled same-target layer on fresh held-out taxa, derive exact decision consequences of collapsing invalid observations into zero, and use prospectively frozen cross-role screens to locate the point at which real ecological systems cease to be answerable from existing data.

## 2. A prospective relation architecture

### 2.1 Freeze the relation before focal comparison

The framework freezes five objects before focal comparison values are opened: the biological relation, the event or relation space in which it applies, adapters mapping role-specific answers into that space, answer-adequacy gates and the calibration or decision rule used to interpret the relation.

We distinguish five relation levels. **Level A** compares the same target reconstructed from independent observation sources. **Level B** compares different roles expected to show soft biological concordance. **Level C** tests directional dependency, `Y requires X`. **Level D** extends this to mutual dependency, potentially with different events in the two directions. **Level E** concerns life-stage coupling. The ladder does not rank ecological importance; it ranks the additional structure that must be justified before a comparison is interpretable.

For Level A, holding target, estimand, estimator family, accessible-area semantics and comparison rows fixed is a deliberate experimental control. Observation source is the focal perturbation. For Levels B–E, forcing the same estimator, predictor set, accessible area or raw output scale can be biologically inappropriate. Each role is therefore estimated using its own defensible model and only then projected onto a common relation space such as plant site × flowering window, host patch × developmental interval or larval site × transition window.

### 2.2 Soft coherence and hard dependency are different tests

Soft relations quantify discordance after valid adaptation and require relation-specific calibration. A source-to-source discordance distribution from a same-target experiment is therefore not a universal tolerance for plant–pollinator, host-dependent or life-stage comparisons.

Hard dependency instead asks whether a mandatory implication is contradicted at an adequately observed event key. We write the general directional relation as

`E(k) -> F(k)`,

where `E(k)` is the dependent biological event and `F(k)` is the required function at the same frozen opportunity key. A hard violation requires both an adequately positive `E(k)` and an adequately negative `F(k)`.

### 2.3 Functions rather than unjustified named-provider exclusivity

The required side of a dependency is defined at the function level whenever multiple providers can satisfy the same biological requirement. Effective pollination, compatible host resource or accessible prey can be supplied by one or several taxa. Provider occurrence, richness, geographic overlap or suitability is not automatically the function itself.

This prevents an obligate process from being translated into a stronger claim that one named provider species must occur wherever the dependent taxon occurs. A directly measured aggregate function and a complete set of alternative providers can be equivalent under a frozen mapping, but an incomplete provider set cannot establish functional absence merely because every represented member is absent.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Controlled same-target cross-source experiment

We constructed independent ecological answers for the same taxa from preserved-specimen and human-observation records. Within each procedure-by-accessible-area cell we held fixed biological target, ecological estimand, the active `bio1-bio19` predictor universe, estimator/procedure identity, accessible-area semantics, an exact 2,000-row comparison frame, answer-adequacy logic and the successor-derived reference rule.

The focal held-out outcome was not used to choose procedures, accessible areas, taxa or rescue paths. A repaired successor audit sealed 2,255/2,256 fit cells, retaining the unresolved cell rather than dropping or repairing it after inspection. The predeclared rule required at least 30 prospectively adequate successor taxa per procedure-by-accessible-area cell. All 24 reference cells met that requirement and were frozen with the unchanged nearest-rank q95 rule before held-out paired discordance was opened.

The held-out matrix contained 12 taxa × 3 accessible-area definitions × 8 procedures = 288 cells. A cell was eligible for the cross-source check only if both independently constructed answers passed their predeclared adequacy gates. Discordance was `1 - Schoener D`, evaluated against the frozen procedure-by-accessible-area reference ceiling.

### 3.2 Prospective Level-C evidence architecture

Moving to directional dependency required three evidence channels to be separated prospectively: external biological evidence defining the relation (`R`), an independent observation stream for the dependent event (`X`) and an independent observation stream for the required function (`Y`). Independence was defined by observation generation rather than publication labels.

Candidate discovery was finite and response-blind. Candidate caps, gate order, prior-system re-entry rules and no-replacement rules were frozen before candidate source inspection. Later versions replaced single-provider exclusivity with function-level semantics and then required a common event key, direct role-specific estimands and provenance separation.

No unavailable source, failed download, missing record or uncalibrated zero was permitted to count as biological absence. Focal Level-C cross-role values remained closed throughout the pre-field sequence.

### 3.3 Negative-state identifiability

For a hard dependency test, we distinguish four states on the required-function side: detected function, calibrated functional absence, unresolved non-detection and observation failure/missingness. Only calibrated functional absence can supply `F(k)=false`.

The prospective calibration floor requires key-level sensitivity of at least 0.80, hard false-negative rate no greater than 0.20 and specificity of at least 0.95, together with explicit quality control and missingness separation. Passing calibration authorizes only a later, separately frozen endpoint-opening stage; it is not itself a biological result.

During the pre-field audit, we identified a contract–implementation mismatch in the frozen v8 operational package: the contract required a one-sided 95% exact-or-more-conservative confidence rule, whereas the v8 evaluator used a Wilson score lower bound. Because no field-calibration data had been entered, v8 was preserved as immutable history and a separate v8.1 repair was prospectively frozen using exact one-sided Clopper–Pearson bounds, with candidate identities, thresholds, minimum counts and missingness rules unchanged.

### 3.4 Exact benchmark of zero-collapsing decisions

We compared two endpoint decision rules on synthetic event keys with known latent truth. The **zero-collapsing comparator** treats invalid observation or a valid non-detection as `F=false` when `E=true`. The **identifiability-gated rule** permits a hard negative only when the observation process passes the frozen calibration floor and the key itself has complete, nonmissing, nonfailed observation; otherwise the relevant zero remains unresolved. The comparator is a decision rule, not a characterization of modern occupancy modelling.

Let `r` be the probability of a true functional event in one observation opportunity, `s` the probability of detecting a true event and `n` the number of opportunities. Conditional on at least one true event existing in the key, key-level sensitivity is

`q = [1 - (1 - r s)^n] / [1 - (1 - r)^n]`.

Let `a` be the probability that an event key is valid for negative inference: complete, nonmissing and nonfailed. For a biologically compatible key with `F=true`, the zero-collapsing false-violation probability is

`FPR_zero = 1 - a q`,

while, if the process qualifies, the gated false-violation probability is

`FPR_gated = a(1-q)`.

Therefore

`FPR_zero - FPR_gated = 1-a`.

For a true violation with `F=false` and key-level specificity `sp`,

`TPR_zero = 1 - a(1-sp)`

and

`TPR_gated = a sp`,

so

`TPR_zero - TPR_gated = 1-a`.

The same invalid-key mass therefore creates both the zero-collapsing rule's apparent sensitivity advantage and an equal increment in false violations.

We evaluated these expressions exactly, without Monte Carlo error, over 8,748 combinations of true violation prevalence `π`, event rarity, event-detection sensitivity, number of opportunities, complete-window fraction, missingness, failure rate and specificity. No parameter was estimated from unopened Level-C focal values.

For a qualified process on valid keys, the false-discovery fraction among called violations is

`FDF = (1-π)(1-q) / [(1-π)(1-q) + π sp]`.

This quantity is independent of invalid-key mass because invalid keys are withheld by the gated rule. It makes explicit that process qualification and endpoint-level evidentiary reliability are different questions.

### 3.5 Pre-data calibration operating-characteristic audit

We separately evaluated the fixed v8.1 calibration decision using exact binomial probabilities. At the minimum sample sizes, a one-sided 95% exact lower bound above the frozen threshold requires at least 28/30 known-positive successes for sensitivity and 60/60 known-negative successes for specificity. These calculations are outcome-blind design diagnostics, not candidate-specific performance estimates. They also assume independent calibration units; clustering must be defined in the candidate-specific field design before collection.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Fresh held-out cross-source reproducibility

Both independent Level-A answers passed their adequacy gates in 283 of 288 held-out cells. The other five remained closed. All 283 opened cells remained within the corresponding frozen successor-derived source-discordance ceiling and no cell triggered `attention_required`.

The closest opened cell to its ceiling was *Nothofagus betuloides* under the 150-km accessible area and the `predictive_forward|logit_l2_C1_degree2` procedure. Observed discordance was 0.38625 against a frozen ceiling of 0.39085. The result supports same-target cross-source reproducibility conditional on answer adequacy, not exact source invariance or unlimited robustness.

### 4.2 Cross-role architecture can succeed while hard absence remains unidentified

An initial 12-candidate function-level Level-C screen produced no fully admitted endpoints. Seven candidates failed the exact event-to-function relation gate, three passed relation, space, time and estimand gates but stopped at focal evidence independence, one could not freeze the exact relation population/site and one was excluded because it had already been screened.

A subsequent prospectively frozen three-channel screen evaluated 12 fresh candidates and identified two architecture-qualified systems. `CREMV3-007` (*Cremastra appendiculata* var. *variabilis*) combined external breeding-system evidence with same-inflorescence fruit outcome and pollinia-carrying camera observations. `BELV3-012` (*Belonocnema treatae* / live-oak budbreak) combined independent host-specificity evidence with natural adult emergence and live-oak budbreak observations.

Both systems passed source materialization, common-key alignment, direct event/function estimands, provenance separation and no-post-hoc-redefinition gates. Both stopped before focal biological opening because their negative function states were not independently calibrated. For Cremastra, a positive pollinia-carrying contact is directly informative, but camera non-detection has not been validated as absence of effective pollination. For Belonocnema, emergence and budbreak are separately observed, but an unrecorded usable-resource state cannot yet be distinguished from incomplete observation.

Candidate-specific calibration audits found no independent stream capable of estimating the required sensitivity and specificity without conditioning on focal outcomes. A subsequent audit of identified supplementary and raw sources also failed to reconstruct such a stream. The admissible next information is prospective calibration measurement, not further candidate hunting or reinterpretation of zeros.

### 4.3 Invalid observation creates an exact inflation term

The exact benchmark contained 4,320 calibration-passing and 4,428 calibration-failing parameter combinations. Across every passing combination,

`FPR_zero - FPR_gated = 1-a`

and

`TPR_zero - TPR_gated = 1-a`

held to floating-point error below `1.2 × 10^-16`.

This separates two sources of error. Imperfect detection on otherwise valid keys contributes `a(1-q)` even after calibration. Invalid observation contributes an additional `1-a` only when it is collapsed into zero. Under the frozen `q >= 0.80` qualification floor, gated key-level false-violation probability was never greater than 0.20 across the grid. By contrast, the zero-collapsing false-violation probability reached 0.776 among the same calibration-passing process conditions because incomplete, missing and failed keys were counted as biological negatives.

With near-perfect valid-key detection but 30% missingness, zero-collapsing false-violation probability was approximately 0.300001 whereas the gated probability was approximately `1.1 × 10^-6`; the missing 30% became unresolved. With 20% observation failure the corresponding probabilities were approximately 0.200001 and `1.3 × 10^-6`. With only half of event keys receiving complete-window observation they were approximately 0.500001 and `7.9 × 10^-7`.

In a mixed qualified regime with key sensitivity 0.8653, specificity 0.95 and valid-key fraction 0.684, zero-collapsing false-violation probability was 0.4081 and gated false-violation probability was 0.0921. Their difference, 0.316, exactly matched the invalid-key mass. Apparent true-violation sensitivity increased by the same 0.316 under zero collapsing; that gain therefore came entirely from treating invalid observations as violations.

When calibration failed, the gated rule made no hard calls. At key sensitivity 0.50 with otherwise complete observation, for example, the zero-collapsing comparator had false-violation probability 0.50 whereas the gated rule retained the relevant non-detections as unresolved.

### 4.4 Calibration pass is not false-discovery control

The observation-process gate controls whether a negative state is eligible to enter a hard endpoint. It does not guarantee that a called violation is probably a true biological violation. For example, with true violation prevalence `π=0.10`, key sensitivity `q=0.80` and specificity `sp=0.99`, the false-discovery fraction among gated calls is approximately 0.645 even with complete valid-key observation.

This does not invalidate the calibration threshold. It defines its role: calibration is an opening condition. The evidentiary interpretation of one or more called violations must be specified separately, including any replication, aggregation or posterior decision rule, before focal Level-C values are opened.

### 4.5 Minimum calibration counts are feasibility minima, not power guarantees

Under v8.1, the minimum 30 known-positive units require at least 28 successes. If true sensitivity is 0.90, the nominal probability of clearing the exact confidence rule at that minimum is 0.411; if true sensitivity is 0.95 it is 0.812. The minimum 60 known-negative units require 60/60 specificity successes. The nominal pass probability at true specificity 0.99 is therefore `0.99^60 = 0.547`, rising to 0.942 only at true specificity 0.999.

These operating characteristics do not authorize sample-size changes. They show why any decision to collect more than the frozen minima must be made prospectively before outcomes are inspected rather than as a rescue after failure.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 The method operates above individual ecological estimators

The occupancy literature already provides principled ways to estimate latent state from repeat or otherwise informative sampling, multispecies models accommodate co-occurrence under imperfect detection, and interaction-network models distinguish missed interactions from true non-occurrence. Model-adequacy frameworks likewise ask whether model structure and data support intended inference (Getz et al. 2018). Our framework does not compete with those approaches. A detection-aware model, adequacy analysis or preregistered workflow can be a component inside it.

The contribution lies in **relation choice and endpoint authorization**. Statistical co-occurrence, predictive similarity and hard biological dependency are not interchangeable claims. The framework requires the intended relation to be specified before focal comparison, permits different estimators upstream, separates the evidence that defines a relation from the streams used to test it, and asks whether the state required to contradict that relation is identified by the observation design.

Prospectivity is important here because it prevents an endpoint from being redefined after the failure mode is known, but prospectivity itself is not the novelty claim. Registered Reports and adaptive preregistration already provide stronger general mechanisms for outcome-blind ecological research. Our narrower contribution is the object being frozen: a relation endpoint between independently constructed ecological answers.

### 5.2 Cross-validity is empirically achievable but does not license cross-role inference

The Level-A experiment shows that prospectively calibrated independent-answer checking can produce a fresh empirical result. Every adequately observable held-out comparison satisfied its frozen source-discordance ceiling, while inadequate cells stayed outside the test. This is stronger than demonstrating that two models can be fitted, but it should not be confused with the invention of independent external validation, which is already established in species-distribution modelling.

What Level A contributes to this framework is a controlled demonstration that a predeclared relation between independently reconstructed answers can close without using focal outcomes to tune procedure, accessible area or tolerance. It also demonstrates the importance of preserving answer nonexistence as unresolved rather than forcing every cell into agreement or disagreement.

Cross-role inference is harder because it changes both the relation and the meaning of disagreement. The appropriate common object is often not a raw surface but an event-key relation such as successful reproduction requiring effective pollination during the same flowering opportunity.

### 5.3 Preserving unresolved states has a measurable inferential value

The synthetic benchmark turns a familiar warning about non-detection into an endpoint-level error decomposition. Under a qualified process, the extra apparent sensitivity obtained by collapsing invalid keys into zero is exactly `1-a`, the mass of keys that are not valid for negative inference. The same mass is added to false violations. That apparent gain therefore cannot be interpreted as biological information.

The remaining error on valid keys, `a(1-q)`, is different. It is genuine observation-process uncertainty and must be controlled by calibration and, where the eventual scientific claim requires it, by a separately frozen aggregation or replication rule. The current sensitivity and specificity floors are opening criteria, not universal evidentiary standards for a single-key biological contradiction.

The base-rate result sharpens this point. Even a qualified observation process can yield a high false-discovery fraction when true violations are rare relative to residual false negatives. Thus three stages must remain separate: observation-process qualification, focal endpoint opening and final evidentiary interpretation.

### 5.4 The stopping rule is scientific, not merely procedural

The Level-C sequence illustrates a practical stopping rule. Once relation definition, event-key alignment, evidence independence and positive role estimands are adequate, but the negative function state remains indistinguishable from non-observation, additional fitting of the same data cannot complete the hard inference unless the sampling design itself contains information capable of identifying that state.

This boundary is not a negative biological result. Neither retained system has been shown to violate or satisfy its dependency. Instead, the framework identifies what new information is required before that question becomes answerable. That is why unavailable calibration remains a measurement requirement rather than being converted into evidence for or against the biology.

### 5.5 Generality

The same architecture applies beyond the motivating systems. Host-dependent development requires a compatible resource during the developmental opportunity, not generic host occurrence. Trophic dependency concerns accessible prey during a relevant foraging opportunity, not equality of predator and prey distributions. Life-stage coupling concerns transition-compatible habitat at the transition event, not identical adult and juvenile niches. Sensor-based interaction studies require calibrated observation before an unobserved interaction can act as a hard negative. Distribution-based process attribution must distinguish lack of environmental support from failure to observe the process itself.

Across these cases, the workflow is the same: construct role-appropriate answers independently, map them to the relation where biology makes a prediction, preserve unresolved observation states, and verify that the state needed to contradict the relation is identifiable before allowing hard inference.

## 6. Conclusion

Ecological answer checking becomes defensible when reproducibility, biological concordance and hard dependency are treated as different inferential levels. A controlled same-target relation can be calibrated and validated prospectively, as demonstrated by the fresh Level-A held-out result. Hard cross-role inference requires more: the negative state that would falsify the dependency must itself be an observable, calibrated state.

The exact benchmark shows why this requirement matters. Collapsing invalid observation into zero creates an apparent sensitivity advantage exactly equal to the invalid-observation mass and adds that same mass to false violations. Preserving unresolved states therefore removes a structurally identifiable source of false biological evidence. Calibration then determines whether a hard negative is admissible; it does not, by itself, guarantee the evidentiary reliability of a called violation.

The practical implication is a stopping rule. When negative-state identifiability is absent, the next scientific step is not automatically a more elaborate model of the same observations. It is a sampling or measurement design that can identify the missing state.
