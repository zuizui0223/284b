# Prospective validation of ecological answers: from cross-source reproducibility to identifiable biological absence

**Stage:** pre-field manuscript v0.6

**Pre-data implementation repair:** Level-C v8.1 exact-confidence evaluator

## Abstract

Ecological answers are increasingly combined across observation sources and biological roles, yet reproducibility and dependency require different evidence. We introduce a prospective relation-endpoint contract that freezes the relation, biological event space, role-specific adapters, adequacy gates and opening rule before focal outcomes are viewed. In a controlled same-target test, 283/283 adequately observed held-out cells reconstructed from specimen and human-observation records remained within frozen source-discordance ceilings; five inadequate cells remained unresolved. For hard dependency `E(k) -> F(k)`, the negative state `F(k)=false` must itself be identifiable. Across 8,748 exact synthetic scenarios, collapsing incomplete, missing or failed keys into zero increased apparent violation sensitivity and false-violation probability by the same amount, `1-a`, where `a` is the valid-key fraction. Two prospective cross-role systems remained sealed because functional absence lacked independent calibration. The method separates answer reproducibility from biological dependency and identifies when new measurement is required.

## 1. Introduction

Ecological synthesis increasingly combines answers generated by different observation processes. Species distributions are reconstructed from museum specimens, opportunistic observations, structured surveys and sensors; interacting taxa are represented by different streams; and host, pollinator, consumer and life-stage responses are estimated at different spatial and temporal grains. Independent data can strengthen external evaluation, while specimen and observation records can differ substantially in sampling process (Matutini et al. 2021; Erickson & Smith 2021; Gaier & Resasco 2023). The inferential problem is therefore not only whether multiple answers can be constructed, but what relation those answers are scientifically entitled to test.

The ingredients are established. Occupancy models separate latent ecological state from observation (MacKenzie et al. 2004; Guillera-Arroita 2017), multispecies models accommodate co-occurrence under imperfect detection (Rota et al. 2016), and interaction work distinguishes missed interactions from non-occurrence (Weinstein & Graham 2017). Getz et al. (2018) made model adequacy and data determinacy explicit methodological concerns. Most directly, Chadwick et al. (2024) systematized observation-process problems through LIES—Latency, Identifiability, Effort and Scale. Preregistration and Registered Reports separately establish outcome-blind design. We therefore do **not** claim novelty for imperfect detection, observation-process identifiability, model adequacy, external validation or prospectivity.

Our object is the **relation endpoint between independently generated ecological answers**. Two reconstructions of the same target can be compared as a controlled cross-source problem because target and estimand can be held fixed. Different biological roles generally cannot be required to have identical suitability surfaces, fitted values or accessible areas. A perennial plant can persist after local pollinator loss and an adult life stage can occur outside larval habitat. Cross-role inference instead requires a relation defined at the biological event where dependency applies.

Hard dependency adds a further requirement. If a dependent event `E(k)` requires a biological function `F(k)` at opportunity `k`, then `E(k)=true` with `F(k)=false` can contradict the dependency. But an observational zero can also result from incomplete coverage, imperfect detection, missingness or failure. Detection-aware models can estimate latent state when sampling identifies the observation process; when it does not, a hard contradiction cannot acquire its negative premise merely through a more elaborate fit.

We develop a relation-endpoint method with three separable tasks: construct adequate role-specific answers, project them into a biological relation space, and verify that any state needed for contradiction is itself identifiable. We demonstrate the controlled same-target layer on fresh held-out taxa, derive exact consequences of collapsing invalid observations into zero, and use prospective cross-role screens to locate the point at which existing data cease to identify the intended hard endpoint.

## 2. Relation-endpoint architecture

### 2.1 Five-part endpoint contract

Before focal comparison values are opened, the method freezes a five-part **relation-endpoint contract**:

1. the biological relation to be tested;
2. the biological event or key space in which it applies;
3. adapters mapping each role-specific answer into that space;
4. answer-adequacy gates determining whether an answer exists;
5. a calibration/opening rule distinguishing admissible relation evidence from unresolved observation.

The contract is estimator-agnostic. An answer can come from an occupancy model, SDM, phenology model, sensor classifier or direct measurement. The endpoint contract does not replace estimator assumptions; it determines what joint relation their outputs may test.

We distinguish **Level A**, same target reconstructed from independent observation sources; **Level B**, different roles expected to show soft concordance; **Level C**, directional dependency; **Level D**, mutual dependency; and **Level E**, life-stage coupling. The ladder ranks required inferential structure rather than ecological importance.

At Level A, holding target, estimand, estimator family, accessible-area semantics and comparison rows fixed is a deliberate control. For Levels B–E, forcing common estimators, predictors, accessible areas or raw scales can be biologically wrong. Each role is therefore constructed defensibly first and then adapted to a common relation space, such as plant site × flowering window or host patch × developmental interval.

### 2.2 Soft coherence and hard dependency

Soft relations quantify discordance after adaptation and require relation-specific calibration. A same-target source-discordance distribution is not a universal tolerance for different roles.

For directional hard dependency we write

`E(k) -> F(k)`,

where `E(k)` is the dependent event and `F(k)` the required function at the same opportunity key. A hard violation requires an adequately positive `E(k)` and an adequately negative `F(k)`.

The required side is defined at the **function** level when several providers can satisfy the same requirement. Effective pollination, compatible host resource or accessible prey need not map to one provider species. Provider occurrence or suitability is not automatically the function, and an incomplete provider set cannot establish functional absence merely because all represented providers are absent.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Controlled same-target cross-source experiment

We constructed independent ecological answers from preserved-specimen and human-observation records. Within each procedure-by-accessible-area cell, target identity, ecological estimand, `bio1-bio19` predictor universe, estimator/procedure identity, accessible-area semantics, an exact 2,000-row comparison frame, adequacy logic and successor-derived reference rule were fixed.

The held-out outcome was not used to select procedures, accessible areas, taxa or rescue paths. A successor audit sealed 2,255/2,256 fit cells and retained the unresolved cell. Each reference cell required at least 30 prospectively adequate successor taxa. All 24 procedure-by-accessible-area cells met that rule and were frozen with the unchanged nearest-rank q95 rule before held-out paired discordance was opened.

The held-out matrix contained 12 taxa × 3 accessible-area definitions × 8 procedures = 288 cells. A cell entered the cross-source check only if both answers passed predeclared adequacy. Discordance was `1 - Schoener D` relative to the frozen reference ceiling.

### 3.2 Prospective Level-C architecture

Directional dependency required three prospectively separated evidence channels: external evidence defining the biological relation (`R`), an independent dependent-event stream (`X`) and an independent required-function stream (`Y`). Independence was defined by observation generation rather than publication label.

Candidate search was finite and response-blind. Candidate caps, gate order, prior-system exclusions and no-replacement rules were frozen before focal source inspection. Function-level semantics replaced unjustified single-provider exclusivity; subsequent gates required a common event key, direct role estimands and provenance separation.

Unavailable sources, failed downloads, missing records and uncalibrated zeros were never permitted to count as biological absence. Focal Level-C cross-role values remained closed throughout the pre-field sequence.

### 3.3 Negative-state identifiability and v8.1 repair

For hard endpoints, we distinguish detected function, calibrated functional absence, unresolved non-detection, and observation failure/missingness. Only calibrated absence can supply `F(k)=false`.

The prospective calibration floor requires key-level sensitivity >=0.80, hard false-negative rate <=0.20 and specificity >=0.95, with explicit quality control and missingness separation. A pass authorizes only a later endpoint-opening stage.

Before any field-calibration data were entered, audit of the frozen v8 operational package found that the contract required a one-sided 95% exact-or-more-conservative confidence rule while the evaluator implemented a Wilson lower bound. v8 was retained as immutable history and v8.1 was prospectively frozen using one-sided exact Clopper–Pearson bounds, with candidate identities, thresholds, minimum counts and missingness rules unchanged.

### 3.4 Exact zero-collapsing benchmark

We compared two endpoint rules on synthetic keys with known latent truth. The **zero-collapsing comparator** treats invalid observation or a valid non-detection as `F=false` when `E=true`. The **identifiability-gated rule** permits a hard negative only when the observation process qualifies and the key is complete, nonmissing and nonfailed; otherwise the zero is unresolved. The comparator is a decision rule, not a characterization of occupancy modelling or LIES.

Let `r` be the true functional-event probability per opportunity, `s` detection probability for a true event and `n` the number of opportunities. Conditional on at least one true event in the key, key-level sensitivity is

`q = [1 - (1 - r s)^n] / [1 - (1 - r)^n]`.

Let `a` be the probability that a key is valid for negative inference. For a compatible key with `F=true`,

`FPR_zero = 1 - a q`

and, for a qualified process,

`FPR_gated = a(1-q)`.

Thus

`FPR_zero - FPR_gated = 1-a`.

For a true violation with `F=false` and key-level specificity `sp`,

`TPR_zero = 1 - a(1-sp)`,

`TPR_gated = a sp`,

and therefore

`TPR_zero - TPR_gated = 1-a`.

The apparent sensitivity gain from coercing invalid keys into zero is therefore matched exactly by added false violations.

We evaluated these expressions without Monte Carlo error over 8,748 combinations of true violation prevalence `π`, event rarity, event-detection sensitivity, number of opportunities, complete-window fraction, missingness, failure rate and specificity. No parameter came from unopened Level-C focal values.

For a qualified process on valid keys, the false-discovery fraction among called violations is

`FDF = (1-π)(1-q) / [(1-π)(1-q) + π sp]`.

This separates process qualification from endpoint-level evidentiary reliability.

### 3.5 Calibration operating characteristics

We evaluated the fixed v8.1 calibration decision using exact binomial probabilities before field data. At the minimum sample sizes, a one-sided 95% exact lower bound above the thresholds requires at least 28/30 known-positive sensitivity successes and 60/60 known-negative specificity successes. These are outcome-blind design diagnostics, not candidate-specific estimates, and assume independent calibration units.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Fresh Level-A closure

Both Level-A answers passed adequacy in 283 of 288 held-out cells; five stayed closed. All 283 opened cells remained within the corresponding frozen successor-derived ceiling and none triggered `attention_required`.

The closest opened cell was *Nothofagus betuloides* under the 150-km accessible area and `predictive_forward|logit_l2_C1_degree2`: discordance was 0.38625 versus a frozen ceiling of 0.39085. The result supports same-target cross-source reproducibility **conditional on answer adequacy**, not exact source invariance.

### 4.2 Level-C architecture reaches a measurement boundary

An initial 12-candidate function-level screen yielded no fully admitted endpoints. Seven candidates failed the exact event-to-function relation gate, three passed relation/space/time/estimand gates but stopped at focal evidence independence, one could not freeze the exact relation population/site and one was excluded as previously screened.

A subsequent frozen three-channel screen evaluated 12 fresh candidates and retained two architecture-qualified systems. `CREMV3-007` (*Cremastra appendiculata* var. *variabilis*) combined external breeding-system evidence, same-inflorescence fruit outcome and pollinia-carrying camera observations. `BELV3-012` (*Belonocnema treatae* / live-oak budbreak) combined independent host-specificity evidence, natural adult emergence and live-oak budbreak observations.

Both passed source materialization, common-key alignment, direct event/function estimands, provenance separation and no-post-hoc-redefinition gates. Both stopped before focal opening because functional absence was not independently calibrated. Positive pollinia-carrying contacts are directly informative for Cremastra, but camera non-detection is not validated as absence of effective pollination. Belonocnema emergence and budbreak are separately observed, but an unrecorded usable-resource state cannot yet be distinguished from incomplete observation.

Candidate-specific calibration audits found no independent stream sufficient to estimate required sensitivity and specificity without conditioning on focal outcomes. Identified supplementary/raw sources also failed to reconstruct such calibration. The next admissible information is prospective measurement, not candidate hunting or reinterpretation of zeros.

### 4.3 Invalid-key mass creates an exact inflation term

The benchmark contained 4,320 calibration-passing and 4,428 calibration-failing parameter combinations. Across every passing combination,

`FPR_zero - FPR_gated = 1-a`

and

`TPR_zero - TPR_gated = 1-a`

held to floating-point error below `1.2 × 10^-16`.

Under the frozen `q >= 0.80` floor, gated key-level false-violation probability was never greater than 0.20 across the grid. Zero-collapsing false-violation probability reached 0.776 among the same passing process conditions because invalid keys were counted as biological negatives.

With near-perfect valid-key detection but 30% missingness, zero-collapsing false-violation probability was approximately 0.300001 while the gated value was approximately `1.1 × 10^-6`; 30% remained unresolved. With 20% observation failure the corresponding probabilities were approximately 0.200001 and `1.3 × 10^-6`. With only half of keys receiving complete-window observation they were approximately 0.500001 and `7.9 × 10^-7`.

In a mixed qualified regime with `q=0.8653`, `sp=0.95` and `a=0.684`, zero-collapsing false-violation probability was 0.4081 and gated probability 0.0921. Their difference, 0.316, exactly matched invalid-key mass; apparent true-violation sensitivity increased by the same 0.316.

When calibration failed, the gated rule made no hard calls. At `q=0.50` with otherwise complete observation, the comparator had false-violation probability 0.50 while the gated rule retained non-detections as unresolved.

### 4.4 Calibration is not false-discovery control

The gate controls whether a negative state is eligible to enter a hard endpoint; it does not guarantee that a called violation is probably a true violation. With `π=0.10`, `q=0.80` and `sp=0.99`, for example, the false-discovery fraction among gated calls is approximately 0.645 even with complete valid-key observation.

Calibration is therefore an endpoint-opening condition. Replication, aggregation or posterior evidence rules must be specified separately before focal Level-C values are opened.

### 4.5 Minimum calibration counts are feasibility minima

At the v8.1 minimum, 30 known-positive units require at least 28 successes. If true sensitivity is 0.90, nominal pass probability is 0.411; at 0.95 it is 0.812. Sixty known-negative units require 60/60 specificity successes; pass probability at true specificity 0.99 is `0.99^60 = 0.547`, increasing to 0.942 at specificity 0.999.

These calculations do not authorize adaptive sample-size extension. Any larger design must be chosen prospectively rather than used as rescue after observed failure.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 The method operates above observation-model choice

LIES is the closest conceptual antecedent: Chadwick et al. (2024) explicitly systematize observation-process identifiability. Occupancy models estimate latent state under informative sampling, interaction models distinguish missed interactions from non-occurrence, and adequacy frameworks ask whether data support an intended model (Getz et al. 2018). The relation-endpoint method can contain any of these components.

Its additional object is **relation choice and endpoint authorization across independently generated answers**. Statistical co-occurrence, predictive similarity and hard biological dependency are different claims. A well-characterized observation process does not determine which relation should hold between two role-specific answers. The endpoint contract fixes the common biological event and the rule by which individually defensible answers may compose into a joint claim.

Prospectivity prevents the endpoint from being redefined after a failure is observed, but prospectivity itself is not the novelty claim. The narrower contribution is the five-part relation-endpoint contract and its opening logic.

### 5.2 Cross-validity does not license cross-role inference

Level A demonstrates that a predeclared relation between independent reconstructions can close empirically without using held-out outcomes to tune procedure, accessible area or tolerance. It also demonstrates why answer nonexistence should remain unresolved rather than being forced into agreement or disagreement.

This is not the invention of external validation. Its role is an empirical anchor for the relation-endpoint method. Cross-role inference requires a different relation and usually a different common object—for example, successful reproduction requiring effective pollination during the same flowering opportunity rather than overlap of adult distribution surfaces.

### 5.3 Unresolved states have measurable inferential value

The benchmark turns the familiar warning about non-detection into endpoint-level decision performance. Under a qualified process, the apparent sensitivity gained by collapsing invalid keys into zero is exactly `1-a`; the same quantity is added to false violations. That gain is therefore not biological information.

Residual error on valid keys, `a(1-q)`, is different: it is genuine observation-process uncertainty. Calibration controls eligibility of a hard negative, while final evidence may require replication or aggregation. The base-rate result shows why these stages must remain distinct: even a qualified process can produce a high false-discovery fraction when true violations are rare.

### 5.4 A scientific stopping rule

The Level-C sequence illustrates the stopping rule. Once relation definition, event-key alignment, evidence independence and positive estimands are adequate, but the negative function state remains indistinguishable from non-observation, additional fitting of the same data cannot complete the hard inference unless the sampling design contains information that identifies that state.

This is not a negative biological result. Neither retained system has been shown to satisfy or violate its dependency. The method instead identifies what new information is required before the question becomes answerable.

### 5.5 Generality

The same architecture extends beyond the motivating systems. Host-dependent development requires a compatible resource during a developmental opportunity, not generic host occurrence. Trophic dependency concerns accessible prey at a relevant foraging opportunity, not equality of predator and prey distributions. Life-stage coupling concerns transition-compatible habitat at a transition event. Sensor-based interaction studies require calibrated observation before non-observation can act as a hard negative.

Across cases, the procedure is the same: construct role-appropriate answers independently, map them to the relation where biology makes a prediction, preserve unresolved states, and verify that the state needed to contradict the relation is identifiable before allowing hard inference.

## 6. Conclusion

Reproducibility, soft biological concordance and hard dependency are different ecological relation endpoints. Same-target cross-source reproducibility can be calibrated and tested prospectively, as the fresh Level-A result demonstrates. Hard cross-role inference requires the state that would falsify the dependency to be observable and calibrated.

The exact benchmark shows that collapsing invalid observation into zero creates an apparent sensitivity advantage equal to invalid-key mass and adds that same mass to false violations. Preserving unresolved states removes this structurally identifiable source of false evidence. Calibration determines whether a hard negative is admissible; it does not guarantee the evidentiary reliability of a called violation.

When negative-state identifiability is absent, the next scientific step is not automatically a more elaborate model of the same observations. It is a measurement design that can identify the missing state.
