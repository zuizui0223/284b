# Title audit v0.1 — relation-endpoint Method paper

Purpose: choose a Stage-1 Ecology Letters working title that foregrounds the actual methodological object after the relation-layer separation audit, without changing the frozen scientific claim boundary.

## Current title

**Prospective validation of ecological answers: from cross-source reproducibility to identifiable biological absence**

### Strengths

- accurately reflects two major components: Level-A cross-source validation and Level-C negative-state identifiability;
- does not overclaim a completed Level-C biological result;
- signals prospectivity and validation.

### Weaknesses after reviewer hardening

- the new methodological object, **relation endpoints**, is not named;
- the title reads like a two-stage empirical story rather than a general inferential Method;
- `identifiable biological absence` overweights the hard-negative branch and underrepresents soft relations, JSDM/data-fusion boundaries and the five-part executable contract;
- `prospective validation` is established methodological language and therefore does not foreground the paper's most specific novelty.

## Candidate titles

### A — selected

**Relation endpoints for ecological inference: when independent answers can support joint biological claims**

Why selected:

1. names the proposed methodological object immediately;
2. states the practical question in ordinary ecological language;
3. spans Level A through hard cross-role relations without implying that Level C is empirically solved;
4. accommodates marginal models, JSDMs, data fusion, sensors and direct measurements as upstream answer generators;
5. fits the formal separation result: individually correct answers do not automatically determine a joint biological relation;
6. avoids implying that Fréchet-Hoeffding bounds, imperfect detection, held-out validation or non-detection logic are the main novelty.

### B

**Relation-endpoint contracts for ecological inference: prospectively authorizing joint biological claims**

Advantage: more explicitly method-like and names the executable contract.

Reason not selected: `authorizing` and `contracts` together can sound governance-heavy before the reader knows the biological problem; it is better suited to methods text than the title.

### C

**From ecological answers to biological relations: a prospective endpoint contract**

Advantage: short and accessible.

Reason not selected: less explicit that the problem concerns multiple independent answers and less searchable by the proposed term `relation endpoints`.

### D

**When ecological answers can be combined: relation endpoints for joint biological inference**

Advantage: intuitive.

Reason not selected: `combined` risks conflation with data fusion, which is an established upstream literature the paper explicitly distinguishes from downstream biological relation authorization.

## Collision / terminology check

Web searches on 12 September 2026 for exact or near-exact phrases `"Relation endpoints for ecological inference"` and `"relation-endpoint contract" ecology` did not identify an established ecological Method using this title/object. Searches did return established ecological **assessment endpoints** in risk assessment, which are a different concept. The manuscript should therefore define `relation endpoint` explicitly at first use and avoid implying priority over ecological assessment-endpoint terminology.
