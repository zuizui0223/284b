## Figure 2. Cross-source consistency across 12 plant taxa and the repaired GBIF input

**Panel A.** Maximum ratio of source-paired discordance (1 − Schoener D) to the corresponding empirical q95 reference threshold is plotted for each of the same 12 plant taxa across its *evaluable* procedure-by-M conditions. Circles represent the historical frozen 9,705-object GBIF source (283/288 evaluable diagnostics, 5 unresolved); diamonds represent the retrospectively restored 10,215-object GBIF source (285/288 evaluable, 3 unresolved). Each source-version maximum is normalized by its **own reference q95**, separately recalibrated for the restored 47 reference taxa in all 24 settings before its heldout scores were opened. Movements between points are descriptive source-version changes, **not** estimates of biological change or independent replications. All 12 maxima are below 1 in both versions. The historical maximum is *Nothofagus betuloides* (0.988), and the restored maximum is *Eucalyptus globulus* (0.950).

**Panel B.** The exact 288-key evaluability transition matrix shows that **282** paired comparisons remained evaluable, **1** originally evaluable comparison became unresolved, **3** originally unresolved comparisons became evaluable, and **2** remained unresolved in both source versions. The predeclared requirement to retain **all 283 historically evaluable comparisons failed**, even though neither version has a taxon with a source-discordance exceedance (0/12 for each arm). A cell is unresolved when either independently fitted source model fails the unchanged adequacy criterion. Unresolved cells are neither agreement nor disagreement. The 12 taxa, not the 283/285 repeated diagnostics, are the independent biological replication units. The q95 source-discordance envelopes are not nominal 95% prediction intervals or conventional significance thresholds.

**Alt text:** A paired-marker plot shows 12 plant species, with a blue circle for the historical maximum normalized discrepancy and a gold diamond for the restored-source maximum; all points lie below the reference line at 1. A 2-by-2 state-transition matrix shows 282 comparisons retained, one lost, three gained and two unresolved in both versions. The annotation says the strict original-cell retention criterion failed despite both arms having zero exceedance taxa.

## Figure 3. Exact class-conditional cost of collapsing invalid observation keys into biological zero

The first two panels compare the gated and zero-collapsed false-violation and true-violation rates in the frozen illustrative state-dependent scenario. With `a₁ = P(valid key | F=true)=0.70`, `a₀ = P(valid key | F=false)=0.90`, valid-key sensitivity `q=0.95` and specificity `sp=0.99`, false-violation rate increases from 0.035 to 0.335 and true-violation sensitivity increases from 0.891 to 0.991. The third panel illustrates the exact identities `ΔFPR=1−a₁` and `ΔTPR=1−a₀`. Both increments are `1−a` **only if** validity is class independent (`a₁=a₀=a`). Values are analytical consequences of a specified observation model, not empirical estimates of missingness or ecology.

**Alt text:** Two paired bar charts show that coding invalid keys as zero increases both false-violation calls and apparent true-violation sensitivity. A third panel plots the downward line 1 minus valid-key fraction, marking increments of 0.30 and 0.10 for two different classes. The difference between those marks emphasizes class-dependent validity.

## Figure 4. Qualified-call reliability changes with state-dependent validity

Analytical false-discovery fractions (FDFs) among qualified hard-violation calls are shown against the unconditional true-violation prevalence before the observation validity gate (π_all). The three theoretical curves all use key-level sensitivity q = 0.80 and specificity sp = 0.99. The solid blue curve assumes equal positive class validities (a₁=a₀), in which case validity cancels and π_valid=π_all. The dashed gold curve assumes a₁=0.70 for biologically compatible keys and a₀=0.90 for true-violation keys. The dark dash-dotted curve reverses these fractions (a₁=0.90, a₀=0.70). At π_all=0.10, the respective qualified-call FDFs are **0.645, 0.586 and 0.700**. This is exactly the selection consequence in the general expression FDF = [(1−π_all)a₁(1−q)] / [(1−π_all)a₁(1−q)+π_all a₀ sp], provided the denominator is nonzero. The shorter formula applies with π_all only for a₁=a₀>0; it otherwise requires prevalence conditioned on validity (π_valid). When no qualified violation calls are possible, FDF is undefined rather than zero. These are *analytic illustrations*, not fitted missingness rates, new ecological evidence, or causal estimates.

**Alt text:** Three descending theoretical curves show false-discovery fraction versus true violation prevalence before validity selection. At a population violation prevalence of 0.10, the equal-validity scenario has FDF 0.645, the scenario with relatively more valid true violations has FDF 0.586, and the scenario with relatively more valid nonviolations has FDF 0.700. The figure distinguishes the two biological classes' observation validities and states that all curves are illustrative.
