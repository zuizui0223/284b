# Ecology Letters — unsolicited Method proposal v0.1

## Working title

**When ecological answers can be combined: prospective cross-validity and the identifiability of biological absence**

Alternative titles:

- **Independent ecological answers require identifiable negative states**
- **From cross-source reproducibility to biological dependency: a prospective framework for ecological answer checking**
- **When absence can falsify an ecological dependency**

## Proposed article type

Method

## Core problem

Ecologists increasingly combine answers reconstructed from heterogeneous observation sources, model classes, taxa and biological roles. Yet two distinct questions are often conflated: whether independently reconstructed answers are reproducible, and whether different biological roles satisfy a dependency that external biology says should hold. The former can be tested as a controlled cross-source problem. The latter requires a biologically meaningful relation space and, for hard dependencies, an observation process capable of identifying the negative state that would falsify the relation.

## Advance

We develop a prospective answer-check framework in which the ecological relation, comparison event, relation-space adapter, adequacy gates and calibration rule are frozen before focal outcomes are opened. The framework separates five relation levels from same-target independent-source reconstruction through directional and mutual biological dependency and life-stage coupling. It explicitly allows role-specific estimators, predictors and accessible areas; comparability is created only after the answers are projected onto a common biological event space.

The framework yields a general negative-state identifiability principle for hard dependency inference: observing a dependent event while failing to detect its required function is a valid falsification only when the negative functional state is independently identifiable under a prospectively calibrated observation process. Missingness, retrieval failure, sensor failure or uncalibrated non-detection therefore cannot be promoted to biological absence.

## Empirical demonstration

We first test the controlled case using independently reconstructed ecological answers from preserved-specimen and human-observation records. A successor panel prospectively froze all 24 procedure-by-accessible-area reference cells before a 12-taxon held-out endpoint was opened. Of 288 held-out cells, 283 passed both source-specific adequacy gates and all 283 remained within their frozen source-discordance ceilings; five cells stayed unresolved because one answer failed adequacy. No focal outcome was used to select procedure, accessible area, taxon or rescue path.

We then prospectively stress-test escalation to hard cross-role dependency. Finite candidate screens, source-architecture gates and source qualification identify two systems with independently separated relation, dependent-event and required-function evidence streams. Both stop before focal biological opening because the observation processes do not independently identify functional absence. Audit of existing supplementary and raw sources fails to reconstruct the missing calibration, locating the inferential boundary at new measurement rather than additional candidate search or reinterpretation of non-detection.

## Why this matters broadly

The distinction applies wherever ecological conclusions depend on the absence of a required process or role: pollination, host dependence, trophic interactions, life-stage transitions, occupancy and distribution modelling, sensor-based interaction inference and process attribution. It changes the interpretation of a common pattern: disagreement or non-detection is not automatically biological contradiction. A cross-check is only as strong as the relation that was frozen and the negative state that can actually be observed.

## Expected take-home

A prospective ecological cross-check has three separable requirements: independent answer construction, a biologically justified relation space, and observational identifiability of the states needed to falsify that relation. Same-target reproducibility can be empirically calibrated without implying cross-role dependency; hard biological dependency requires an additional negative-state calibration that cannot be supplied by missingness or unvalidated non-detection.

## Claim boundary

The manuscript does not claim a completed cross-role biological result, a causal process knockout, universal source invariance, or universal transfer of the same-target tolerance. The Level-C systems are used only to demonstrate the prospectively documented inferential boundary before new calibration data are collected.
