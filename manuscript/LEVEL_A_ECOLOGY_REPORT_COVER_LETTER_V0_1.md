# Cover letter draft — Ecology Report

Dear Editors of Ecology,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Cross-source reproducibility of species-distribution predictions across prospectively held-out plant taxa,”** for publication as a Report in Ecology.

Biodiversity models are increasingly built from data streams with sharply different observation processes, including preserved specimens and human observations. Rather than asking whether those sources are interchangeable, our study asks a simple empirical question: when both sources independently estimate the same ecological target, do their distribution predictions remain reproducible on taxa that were not used to define acceptable source-to-source disagreement?

We tested this prospectively. Procedure-by-area discordance envelopes were calibrated from an independent reference panel and frozen before opening outcomes for 12 held-out taxa. The held-out panel generated 288 prespecified diagnostics; 283 were evaluable and five remained unresolved because one source-specific answer did not pass its predeclared adequacy rule. None of the 12 held-out taxa contained an envelope exceedance among evaluable cells. The closest cell approached the frozen boundary closely (observed/envelope = 0.988), so the result is not a consequence of uniformly uninformative bounds.

We believe the Report format fits the study because the paper makes one concise empirical statement: differences in biodiversity-data provenance need not prevent reproducibility of a downstream same-target ecological prediction when the estimand, comparison geometry and evaluation rule are fixed before held-out outcomes are viewed. We deliberately do not claim universal source invariance, mechanism, or a cross-role biological dependency.

The analysis contracts, code and frozen machine-readable endpoint receipts are available in the project repository, and a permanent archival DOI will be supplied before final submission.
