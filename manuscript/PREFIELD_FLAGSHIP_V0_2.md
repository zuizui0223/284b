# Prospective validation of ecological answers: from cross-source reproducibility to identifiable biological absence

**Target:** Ecology Letters — Method  
**Stage:** pre-field manuscript v0.2  
**Frozen empirical boundary:** `bdc81d0`  
**Synthetic benchmark:** `pre_field_identifiability_benchmark_v0_1`  
**Main-text target:** <= 5,000 words  
**Display-item target:** <= 6

## Abstract

Ecologists increasingly combine answers reconstructed from different observation sources and biological roles, but reproducibility and biological dependency are not the same inferential problem. We develop a prospective answer-check framework in which the relation, biological event, relation-space adapter, adequacy gates and calibration are frozen before focal outcomes are opened. In a controlled same-target test, 283/283 adequately observed held-out cells reconstructed independently from preserved-specimen and human-observation records remained within prospectively calibrated source-discordance ceilings; five inadequate cells remained unresolved. We then derive the additional requirement for hard cross-role dependency. A hard implication `E(k) -> F(k)` can be contradicted only when `E(k)` is adequately positive and the negative function state `F(k)=false` is itself identifiable. An exact 8,748-scenario benchmark shows that, for a qualified observation process, collapsing incomplete, missing or failed keys into zero increases both apparent violation sensitivity and false-violation probability by exactly the invalid-key mass. Prospective Level-C screens identified two fresh systems with independent relation, event and function streams, but both stopped before focal biological opening because functional absence was not independently calibrated. The framework separates cross-source reproducibility from cross-role dependency and provides a transferable stopping rule for when additional modelling should give way to new measurement.

## 1. Introduction

Ecological synthesis increasingly combines answers that were not generated by the same observation process. Species distributions are reconstructed from museum specimens, opportunistic observations and sensors; interacting taxa are represented by different data streams; host, pollinator, consumer and life-stage responses are estimated at distinct spatial and temporal grains. Two intuitions then invite overreach. First, independently reconstructed answers should agree if they represent the same ecology. Second, biological roles known to depend on one another should produce mutually compatible answers. These intuitions are related, but they are not the same statistical claim.

Agreement is meaningful only after the expected relation has been specified. Two reconstructions of the same target can be treated as a controlled cross-source problem because target and estimand can be held fixed. Two different biological roles generally cannot be required to have equal fitted values, equal suitability surfaces or the same accessible area. A perennial plant can persist after local pollinator loss, an adult stage can disperse beyond larval habitat, and a dependent consumer can be observed outside the interval in which its resource was measured. Cross-role inference therefore requires a relation defined at the biological event where dependency actually operates.

Hard dependency adds a second problem. Suppose a dependent event `E(k)` requires a biological function `F(k)` at opportunity `k`. Observing `E(k)` together with `F(k)=false` can contradict the proposed dependency. Yet a recorded zero is not automatically `F(k)=false`. It can arise from incomplete temporal coverage, imperfect detection, missing records, sensor failure or observer failure. If such states are collapsed into biological absence, the evidence used to falsify the dependency is partly manufactured by the observation process itself.

We develop a prospective framework that separates these inferential layers before focal outcomes are opened. We first test whether a controlled same-target cross-source relation can close on fresh held-out taxa. We then ask how the requirements change when moving from reproducibility to directional biological dependency. Finally, we derive an exact benchmark comparing a naive zero-as-absence rule with an identifiability-gated rule that preserves unresolved states. The result is a general criterion for deciding when ecological answers can be compared, when a biological dependency can be tested, and when the inferential bottleneck has moved from modelling to measurement.

## 2. Framework

### 2.1 A prospective relation ladder

The framework begins by freezing five objects before focal comparison values are opened: the biological relation, the event or relation space in which it applies, the adapters that map role-specific answers into that space, answer-adequacy gates and any calibration rule used to interpret discordance or absence.

We distinguish five relation levels. Level A compares the same target reconstructed from independent observation sources. Level B compares different roles expected to show soft biological concordance. Level C tests directional dependency, `Y requires X`. Level D extends this to mutual dependency, potentially with different events in the two directions. Level E concerns life-stage coupling. The ladder does not rank ecological importance; it ranks how much additional structure must be justified before a comparison is interpretable.

For Level A, holding target, estimand, estimator family, accessible-area semantics and comparison rows fixed is a deliberate experimental control. Observation source is the focal perturbation. For Levels B-E, forcing the same estimator, predictor set, accessible area or raw output scale can be biologically wrong. Each role is therefore estimated appropriately first and only then projected onto a common relation space such as plant site × flowering window, host patch × developmental interval or larval site × transition window.

### 2.2 Soft coherence and hard dependency

Soft relations quantify discordance after valid adaptation and require relation-specific calibration. A source-to-source discordance distribution from Level A is therefore not a universal tolerance for plant-pollinator, host-dependent or life-stage comparisons.

Hard dependency instead asks whether a mandatory implication is contradicted at an adequately observed event key. We write the general directional relation as

`E(k) -> F(k)`,

where `E(k)` is the dependent biological event and `F(k)` is the required function at the same frozen opportunity key. A hard violation requires both an adequately positive `E(k)` and an adequately negative `F(k)`.

### 2.3 Function rather than named-provider occurrence

The required side of a dependency is defined at the function level whenever multiple providers can satisfy the same biological requirement. Effective pollination, compatible host resource or accessible prey can be supplied by one or several taxa. Provider occurrence, richness, geographic overlap or suitability is not automatically the function itself.

This distinction prevents an obligate process from being translated into an unjustified claim that one named provider species must occupy every place where the dependent taxon occurs. A directly measured aggregate function and a complete set of alternative providers can be equivalent under a frozen mapping, but an incomplete provider set cannot establish functional absence merely because every represented member is absent.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Level-A controlled reconstruction experiment

We constructed independent ecological answers for the same taxa from preserved-specimen and human-observation records. Within each procedure-by-accessible-area cell we held fixed biological target, ecological estimand, active `bio1-bio19` predictor universe, estimator/procedure identity, accessible-area semantics, an exact 2,000-row comparison frame, answer-adequacy logic and the successor-derived reference rule.

The focal held-out outcome was not used to choose procedures, accessible areas, taxa or rescue paths. A repaired successor audit sealed 2,255/2,256 fit cells, retaining the unresolved cell rather than dropping or repairing it after inspection. The predeclared rule required at least 30 prospectively adequate successor taxa per procedure-by-accessible-area cell. All 24 reference cells met that requirement and were frozen with the unchanged nearest-rank q95 rule before held-out paired discordance was opened.

The held-out matrix contained 12 taxa × 3 accessible-area definitions × 8 procedures = 288 cells. A cell was eligible for the cross-source check only if both independently constructed answers passed their predeclared adequacy gates. Discordance was `1 - Schoener D`, evaluated against the frozen procedure-by-accessible-area reference ceiling.

### 3.2 Prospective Level-C candidate architecture

Moving to directional dependency required three evidence channels to be separated prospectively: external biological evidence defining the relation (`R`), an independent observation stream for the dependent event (`X`) and an independent observation stream for the required function (`Y`). Independence was defined by observation generation rather than by publication labels.

Candidate discovery was finite and response-blind. Candidate caps, gate order, prior-system re-entry rules and no-replacement rules were frozen before candidate source inspection. Later versions replaced single-provider exclusivity with function-level semantics and then required a common event key, direct role-specific estimands and provenance separation.

No unavailable source, failed download, missing record or uncalibrated zero was permitted to count as biological absence. Focal Level-C cross-role values remained closed throughout the pre-field sequence.

### 3.3 Negative-state identifiability gate

For a hard dependency test, we distinguish four observation states on the required-function side: detected function, calibrated functional absence, unresolved non-detection and observation failure/missingness. Only calibrated functional absence can supply `F(k)=false`.

The current pre-field calibration floor requires key-level sensitivity of at least 0.80, hard false-negative rate no greater than 0.20 and specificity of at least 0.95, together with explicit QC and missingness separation. Passing this calibration is only an authorization condition for a later separately frozen endpoint-opening stage. It is not itself a biological result and does not imply that a single negative key is sufficient for final dependency falsification.

### 3.4 Exact synthetic benchmark

We compared two decision rules on synthetic event keys with known latent truth.

The **naive zero-as-absence rule** treats a valid non-detection, incomplete observation, missing record or failed observation as `F=false` when `E=true`.

The **identifiability-gated rule** permits a hard negative only when the observation process passes the frozen sensitivity/specificity floor and the key itself has complete, nonmissing, nonfailed observation. Otherwise the relevant zero is `unresolved`.

Let `r` be the probability of a true functional event in one observation opportunity, `s` the probability of detecting a true event, and `n` the number of opportunities. Conditional on at least one true event existing in the key, key-level sensitivity is

`q = [1 - (1 - r s)^n] / [1 - (1 - r)^n]`.

Let `v` be the probability that an event key is valid for negative inference: complete, nonmissing and nonfailed. The benchmark grid generates `v` as `c(1-m)(1-f)` from complete-window fraction `c`, missingness `m` and failure rate `f`, but the core identities below require only the aggregate `v`, not independence among those mechanisms.

For a compatible key with `F=true`, the naive false-violation probability is

`FPR_naive = 1 - v q`.

If the process qualifies, the gated false-violation probability is

`FPR_gated = v(1-q)`.

Hence

`FPR_naive - FPR_gated = 1-v`.

For a true violation with `F=false` and key-level specificity `sp`, the naive true-violation sensitivity is

`TPR_naive = 1 - v(1-sp)`,

whereas the qualified gated rule gives

`TPR_gated = v sp`.

Thus

`TPR_naive - TPR_gated = 1-v`.

The same invalid-key mass therefore creates both the naive rule's apparent sensitivity advantage and an equal increment in false violations.

We evaluated these expressions exactly, without Monte Carlo error, over 8,748 combinations of violation prevalence, event rarity, event-detection sensitivity, number of opportunities, complete-window fraction, missingness, failure rate and specificity. No parameter was estimated from unopened Level-C focal values.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Fresh held-out cross-source reproducibility

Both independent Level-A answers passed their adequacy gates in 283 of 288 held-out cells. The other five remained closed. All 283 opened cells remained within the corresponding frozen successor-derived source-discordance ceiling and no cell triggered `attention_required`.

The closest opened cell to its ceiling was *Nothofagus betuloides* under the 150-km accessible area and the `predictive_forward|logit_l2_C1_degree2` procedure. Observed discordance was 0.38625 against a frozen ceiling of 0.39085. The result therefore supports same-target cross-source reproducibility conditional on answer adequacy, not exact source invariance or unlimited robustness.

### 4.2 Cross-role architecture can succeed while hard absence remains unidentified

An initial 12-candidate function-level Level-C screen produced no fully admitted endpoints. Seven candidates failed the exact event-to-function relation gate, three passed relation, space, time and estimand gates but stopped at focal evidence independence, one could not freeze the exact relation population/site and one was excluded because it had already been screened.

A subsequent prospectively frozen three-channel screen evaluated 12 fresh candidates and identified two architecture-qualified systems. `CREMV3-007` (*Cremastra appendiculata* var. *variabilis*) combined external breeding-system evidence with same-inflorescence fruit outcome and pollinia-carrying camera observations. `BELV3-012` (*Belonocnema treatae* / live-oak budbreak) combined independent host-specificity evidence with natural adult emergence and live-oak budbreak observations.

Both systems passed source materialization, common-key alignment, direct event/function estimands, provenance separation and no-post-hoc-redefinition gates. Both stopped before focal biological opening because their negative function states were not independently calibrated. For Cremastra, a positive pollinia-carrying contact is directly informative, but camera non-detection has not been validated as absence of effective pollination. For Belonocnema, emergence and budbreak are separately observed, but an unrecorded usable-resource state cannot yet be distinguished from incomplete observation.

Candidate-specific calibration audits found no independent stream capable of estimating the required sensitivity and specificity without conditioning on focal outcomes. A subsequent audit of identified supplementary and raw sources also failed to reconstruct such a stream. The admissible next information is prospective calibration measurement, not further candidate hunting or reinterpretation of zeros.

### 4.3 Invalid observation creates an exact inflation term

The exact benchmark contained 4,320 calibration-passing and 4,428 calibration-failing parameter combinations. Across every passing combination, the two identities

`FPR_naive - FPR_gated = 1-v`

and

`TPR_naive - TPR_gated = 1-v`

held to floating-point error below `1.2 × 10^-16`.

This result separates two sources of hard-negative error. Imperfect valid-key sensitivity contributes `v(1-q)` even after calibration. Invalid observation contributes an additional `1-v` only when it is collapsed into zero. Under the frozen `q >= 0.80` qualification floor, the gated key-level false-violation probability was never greater than 0.20 across the full grid. By contrast, the naive false-violation probability reached 0.776 among the same calibration-passing process conditions because incomplete, missing and failed keys were counted as biological negatives.

Representative cases make the decomposition transparent. With near-perfect valid-key detection but 30% missingness, naive false-violation probability was approximately 0.300001 whereas the gated probability was approximately `1.1 × 10^-6`; the missing 30% became unresolved. With 20% device/observer failure, naive false-violation probability was approximately 0.200001 and the gated value approximately `1.3 × 10^-6`. With only half of event keys receiving complete-window observation, the corresponding values were approximately 0.500001 and `7.9 × 10^-7`.

In a mixed qualified regime with key sensitivity 0.8653, specificity 0.95 and valid-key fraction 0.684, naive false-violation probability was 0.4081 and gated false-violation probability was 0.0921. The difference, 0.316, exactly matched the invalid-key mass. Naive true-violation sensitivity exceeded gated sensitivity by the same 0.316; the apparent gain therefore came entirely from counting invalid observations as violations.

When calibration failed, the gated rule made no hard calls. For example, at key sensitivity 0.50 with otherwise complete observation, the naive rule produced a false-violation probability of 0.50 whereas the gated rule returned the relevant non-detections as unresolved.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Cross-validity is empirically achievable, but it does not license cross-role inference

The Level-A experiment shows that prospectively calibrated independent-answer checking can produce a nontrivial fresh empirical result. Every adequately observable held-out comparison satisfied its frozen source-discordance ceiling, while inadequate cells were kept outside the test. This is stronger than demonstrating that two models can be fitted: it establishes held-out cross-validity under a controlled source perturbation.

That result cannot simply be exported to different biological roles. Cross-role inference changes both the relation and the meaning of disagreement. The appropriate common object is often not a raw surface but an event-key relation such as successful reproduction requiring effective pollination during the same flowering opportunity.

### 5.2 Preserving unresolved states has a measurable inferential value

The synthetic benchmark converts a familiar verbal caution—absence of evidence is not evidence of absence—into an auditable error decomposition. Under a qualified process, the extra apparent sensitivity obtained by zero-as-absence is exactly `1-v`, the mass of keys that are not valid for negative inference. The same mass is added to false violations. A naive rule therefore cannot claim that gain as biological information.

This point matters because naive rules can look operationally powerful. Missing or failed keys often produce zeros, so counting them as absence increases the number of apparent violations found. The benchmark shows why that apparent power is deceptive: it is accompanied by an exactly matched structural inflation in false violations.

The remaining error on valid keys, `v(1-q)`, is different. It is genuine observation-process uncertainty and must be controlled by calibration and, where the final scientific claim demands it, by a separately frozen replication or aggregation rule. The current 0.80 sensitivity floor is consequently an opening criterion rather than a universal evidentiary standard for single-key falsification.

### 5.3 The stopping rule is scientific, not merely procedural

The Level-C sequence illustrates a useful stopping rule. Once relation definition, event-key alignment, evidence independence and positive role estimands are adequate, but the negative function state remains indistinguishable from non-observation, additional fitting of the same data cannot complete the hard inference. The missing information is measurement calibration.

This boundary is not a negative biological result. Neither retained system has been shown to violate or satisfy its dependency. Instead, the framework identifies exactly what new information is required before that question is answerable. The current operational package therefore freezes candidate-specific field calibration while keeping focal cross-role values, hard invariants and process interventions closed.

### 5.4 Generality

The same architecture applies broadly. Host-dependent development requires a compatible resource during the developmental opportunity, not generic host occurrence. Trophic dependency concerns accessible prey during a relevant foraging opportunity, not equality of predator and prey distributions. Life-stage coupling concerns transition-compatible habitat at the transition event, not identical adult and juvenile niches. Sensor-based interaction studies require calibrated detection before a zero can indicate absence of interaction. Distribution-based process attribution must distinguish lack of environmental support from failure to observe the process itself.

Across these cases, the workflow is the same: construct role-appropriate answers independently, map them to the relation where biology makes a prediction, preserve unresolved observation states, and verify that the state needed to contradict the relation is identifiable before allowing hard inference.

## 6. Conclusion

Ecological answer checking becomes defensible when reproducibility, biological concordance and hard dependency are treated as different inferential levels. A controlled same-target relation can be calibrated and validated prospectively, as demonstrated by the fresh Level-A held-out result. Hard cross-role inference requires more: the negative state that would falsify the dependency must itself be an observable, calibrated state.

The exact benchmark shows why this requirement matters. Collapsing invalid observation into zero creates an apparent sensitivity advantage that is exactly equal to the invalid-observation mass and adds that same mass to false violations. Preserving unresolved states is therefore not merely cautious bookkeeping; it removes a structurally identifiable source of false biological evidence.

The practical implication is a stopping rule. When negative-state identifiability is absent, the next scientific step is not a more elaborate model of the same observations. It is a better measurement design.
